## Sun Lakes texture (real-world source material)

### The setting
Hotel Sun Lakes, based on The Cove at Sun Lakes (thecoveatsunlakes.org). Four condos plus a big main house, all connected. Shared family dinners. Two docks: the main dock and a back dock off to the side. A small cliff the older kids jump off. Snake Island is NOT in this book. Jeff doesn't want the kids reading about it and then trying to go there. Forgotten entirely.

### The condos (real-world only - no named adults in the story)
- **Main cabin:** Uncle Bill and Kim. Real-world only. NEVER IN STORY.
- **Condo 1:** Carson (15) and Keira (10). Brother and sister. Both super kind and athletic.
- **Condo 2:** Reid (14) and Ella (10).
- **Condo 3:** Madison (15) and Kellen (12).
- **Condo 4:** Aiden (7) and Chayten (13).

### Family structure (matters in the book)
- House + Condo 1 + Condo 2 are blood-related (Uncle Bill family with two related families in 1 and 2). Carson, Keira, Reid, and Ella are first cousins.
- Condo 3 mom and Condo 4 mom are long-time friends with Condo 2 mom. Chosen family.
- All eight kids = chosen family through this once-a-year trip.
- This split (blood vs chosen) is THE thematic center. The villain probably tries to weaponize the difference. The book proves chosen family is no weaker than blood.

### The inflatables room (THE PORTAL)
The SUNROOM. Opens up with a view of the lake. Right next to where the brisket cooks all day (smoke and meat smell drifting in). Weirdly warm because of the sun on the windows and the drying plastic of the inflatables.

Every shape and size of inflatable in there, including a big inflated unicorn. Kids take turns - no one owns a specific raft. Younger kids wear life jackets in the water. Everyone except Aiden gets one night swim at the end of the week. (Aiden being too young for the night swim is the kind of detail that should pay off in his branch - he gets HIS night swim, but in the magical world.)

In-world: kids walk past the sunroom every day all week. One day, something is different.

### Activities (real life on the trip)
- Bike rides, round of golf, miniature golf
- Water balloon launcher: kids vs other team
- Ice cream cones
- Reading on the grass
- Card games. Screw Your Neighbor is the staple (one card per player, swap with your neighbor, lowest card loses a life). The swap/deception mechanic is GOLD - candidate for a magical mechanic where you have to risk what you have for what you think someone else has.
- Girls balance on inflatables on the water and do tricks. Adults judge using made-up gymnastics names. Real examples: "Russian Marinov hold," "straddle flip up twisty too." HAS to be in the book. Probably becomes an actual judging tribunal scene in someone's branch (Chayten? Madison?).
- Boys skip rocks
- Both paddle board
- Older kids jump off the small cliff
- Tubing, boating, sun
- Once-a-year family environment - the trip is the only time all year these kids see each other

### THE UNPLUG RULE (real-world trip value)
The whole point of Sun Lakes for the kids is to UNPLUG. **No video games. No screens.** This is real - it's why the parents drag everyone to the cove every July. The kids spend a week being kids without devices.

This is the thematic spine of the entire book. Driedmore wants to build a DATA CENTER (the most plugged-in thing humans have invented) right where the kids come to UNPLUG. The villain isn't just an environmental threat. He's the embodiment of everything the trip is trying to escape.

The Old Roar is by definition tech-free. It's pre-electric. The kids can be their fullest selves there because there's nothing competing for their attention. The unicorn is the literal opposite of a data center: analog, alive, doesn't fit on a database row.

Drystone's character should reflect this: always on his phone, checking forms on a tablet, scanning QR codes. The kids are face-to-face with each other and with magic. Tech vs presence. The eight kids beat the data center by being PRESENT - in person, in real time, with each other.

### The dock (real-world setting detail)
The dock at Hotel Sun Lakes is real. Its FOUNDATION is being eroded by the water's flow patterns. The decking is fine, but the pilings underneath are losing the ground around them. Hotel can't afford the geological work to stabilize it. This is the opening Driedmore is exploiting - a "rescue" buyout because the family can't fix the foundation.

In-book payoff: The Lake Sisters and Cliffy know why the water flows the way it flows. The kids learn (mostly through Madison's branch and Keira's branch) what's eroding the foundation and what would stop it. In the coda, the foundation has quietly stabilized overnight. Magical residue redirects the flow. Adults don't notice. The dock holds.

### Traditions
- KISS the first fish you catch. Mandatory.
- Fourth of July: sparklers and snap-its (the throwable poppers that bang on the ground). Family-only, intimate, not a big public fireworks show.
- Brisket cooks all day. Smoke and meat smell saturates the sunroom.
- **A C-130 flies low through the canyon multiple times during 4th of July week.** Massive military cargo plane, ground-shaking. Kids feel it in their chests. The first flyover triggers the portal. A later flyover lands at the showdown climax. Multiple flyovers during the week is real - this isn't invented timing.
- **Prizes for tasks.** Real-life trip tradition: kids earn prizes for doing things. Catch a fish, earn a prize. Take the trash out, earn a prize. Massage a parent five minutes, earn a prize. Read for thirty minutes, earn a prize. **THIS IS THE STRUCTURAL ANCESTOR OF THE EIGHT EARNED THINGS.** Each branch is a prize quest. The kid completes a task, earns a prize. Same rhythm the kids already know from the trip.

### Each kid's signature trip activity and trait
- **Madison (15):** Oldest. Parental latitude/authority. Always helping but always with a strategic eye. The one the others answer to without thinking about why. **Also: makes the best cookies.** Branch trait: strategic leadership. Her cookies should travel with her into the magical world.
- **Chayten (13):** The connector. Loves to hang with anyone. Paddle boards with Madison; fishes with her little brother Aiden on the back dock. Fierce Aiden-protector. Branch trait: performance, persuasion, bridging older/younger.
- **Carson (15):** Loves the entire week. Competitive AND fun. Protective older cousin to Aiden (Aiden has TWO Aiden-protectors: Chayten the sister, Carson the cousin). Branch trait: competing without losing kindness.
- **Reid (14):** Gentle giant. Makes a stick from nearby wood every trip. Knowledgeable, calm. Branch trait: strength + craft + applied knowledge.
- **Kellen (12):** Throws a ball constantly. Athletic, hides his intelligence. Branch trait: forced to reveal his mind, not just his body.
- **Keira (10):** Does arts. Tall for her age, competes with the older kids despite being young. Branch trait: noticing what others miss; pattern recognition through the artist's eye.
- **Ella (10):** Puts on makeup. Princess energy, fierce protector. Branch trait: regal courage; standing up to something bigger.
- **Aiden (7):** Wants to fish on the back dock. The youngest. Energetic, kind, gentle, knows right from wrong. Hasn't done the night swim yet. Branch trait: moral compass driving choices; wonder; kindness as power.

## The two worlds

### Real world
Hotel Sun Lakes, present day. The setting from the texture above. The kids hear about a developer who's going to ruin Sun Lakes. That's the real-world threat that frames the whole book.

### The magical world: The Old Roar
Same place, different time. Roughly the era when the Ice Age floods carved this country and water roared through. With magic. The lakes are vast, the falls are huge and loud (Dry Falls is wet again - hence "The Old Roar"), the basalt cliffs are new and shiny, the land is green and alive. Magical creatures live in and around the water. The eight kids cross into The Old Roar through the sunroom and the rafts.

### How the two worlds connect
The magic of the ancient world is what protects modern Sun Lakes. The kids have to go to the magical world, earn what each one earns there, and bring it back to stop the developer in the real world. Each branch ends with the kid earning ONE THING (item, ally, knowledge, power). Eight things together = how they beat the developer.

## How the portal works
The sunroom + the inflatables. The rafts are time machines. Each raft becomes the vehicle a kid rides into the magical world. The big inflated unicorn is one of them. The shape of the raft probably matters in the magical world (a unicorn rider is treated differently than a flamingo rider, etc.).

## How magic crosses back
Pegasus the unicorn comes back with Aiden after his branch. **She is invisible to adults.** Only the eight kids can see her. She lives in the sunroom for the rest of the trip. Adults are mystified that the kids keep talking to a corner. This rule keeps the universe from breaking and gives the eight kids a shared secret that lasts past the book. Strong sequel hook.

## The villain: Mr. Drystone of Driedmore Properties

Foreman for Driedmore Properties. Driedmore wants to BUY Hotel Sun Lakes from the family, drain the cove, and build a DATA CENTER on the dry lakebed. (Real environmental issue: data centers consume massive amounts of water for cooling. Real news, not invented.)

How they're getting in: **the dock is falling apart in real life.** The hotel can't afford to fix it. Driedmore is offering to buy the whole property as a "rescue." Adults are tempted because they don't see another way. The kids see Driedmore for what it is.

The construction kickoff is for the data center, not housing. Same dock setting, same Drystone, same showdown. New stakes: if Driedmore wins, the cove is drained, the lake is gone, the magical world (which is the ancient version of this lake) is also gone, and the family's annual trip ends forever.

Style: clipboard guy played for laughs. Carries three clipboards at all times. Pronounces "ecosystem" "echo-system" and won't be corrected. Says things like "according to subsection 4B" and "this here is a malfeasance of zoning." Wears a hard hat indoors. Sweats when things don't fit on his forms. Underestimates kids out loud.

Why he loses: the magical world isn't on his maps. He cannot process what the kids bring back. The system has no field for "a real live creature from The Old Roar."

He never enters The Old Roar. He's a real-world-only problem.

## The magical world: rules of The Old Roar

1. **The rafts are alive in The Old Roar.** Each kid rides their raft in. The raft becomes their companion/vehicle in the magical world. The big inflated unicorn becomes a real unicorn (Aiden rides this one).
2. **Magic responds to kindness, courage, and cleverness.** Bullying or lying in The Old Roar makes things go wrong. Generosity and good faith make things go right.
3. **Time runs slower in The Old Roar.** A whole adventure for the kids = an afternoon in real time. Built-in safety so we don't have to explain why no one missed them.
4. **Each kid earns ONE THING in their branch.** Item, ally, song, knowledge - something concrete they bring back.
5. **Cookies, music, and stories cross between worlds.** Madison's cookies smell magical to creatures in The Old Roar. Songs the kids sing in The Old Roar still work in real life.

## The eight magical creatures (one per branch, all goofy and warm)

Each creature is FRIENDLY. Each has a running comic tic. Each celebrates the kid they meet.

1. **Madison: Pip, Lark, and Wren - the Lake Sisters.** Three water-girls who can breathe underwater and live in the Quiet Pool. Roughly Madison's age and a little younger. They've been watching Madison bake cookies through the lake water for years (somehow the smell drifts down). They love sweets. They are TERRIBLE at baking (their cookies are made of pondweed and one of them keeps eating the dough raw). Comic tic: they argue about which kind of cookie is best, in the middle of crisis moments.

2. **Chayten: The Driftwood Players.** A traveling troupe of otters who put on plays. They are dramatic, overdramatic, and very proud of their craft. They are also really not very good. Their lead actor (an old otter named Otto) just sprained his whiskers. They cast Chayten because she's the right size for the costume AND has "the eyes for tragedy." Comic tic: every otter is convinced they're the best actor in the troupe.

3. **Carson: Big Coulee.** A wooly mammoth, friendly and giant, who's training for the Coulee Games. She wears a flower crown she will not take off (it's tradition). She has two baby mammoths who keep wandering off. Comic tic: she narrates her own training out loud ("And BIG COULEE rounds the bend, looking STRONG, looking FAST...").

4. **Reid: Old Speak.** A tree-creature shaped like a person. Lives in a rocky woodland. Hasn't had a visitor in a thousand years. Tells terrible jokes and laughs at them himself. Comic tic: he interrupts his own stories to remember a joke he forgot to tell.

5. **Kellen: The Skip-Stones.** A flock of bird-creatures who play the most complicated stone-skipping game in The Old Roar. They are champions and they know it. They mildly trash-talk every challenger. Comic tic: their trash-talk is unbelievably tame ("your form is mediocre," "you couldn't skip a stone in a bathtub").

6. **Keira: The Painted Cliff.** A giant sleeping creature whose face is a basalt cliff and whose arms are the canyon walls. He's been asleep for centuries. When he stirs in his sleep, the rocks SNORE in colors. Only Keira can see the colors at first. Comic tic: when he wakes up, he's confused and very polite, like a giant who just remembered he's late for tea.

7. **Ella: The Queen of the Bright Water.** A kid-queen, age 8, who's been queen for one week and is VERY stressed about it. Has a court of frogs who give her terrible advice and argue about everything. Comic tic: the frogs always disagree, and the queen always asks them anyway.

8. **Aiden: Pegasus.** (Working name. The Unicorn from the inflatable raft, now real.) A young unicorn who's never been ridden because no one has been the right rider before. Excited about absolutely everything. Says "look at THIS!" about every cloud and every flower. Comic tic: she narrates her own life ("And then the unicorn TURNED LEFT, because LEFT was the BEST direction!").

## The eight earned things (used in the showdown)

1. Madison: a map of Driedmore Properties' real plans (the one Drystone keeps in his clipboard)
2. Chayten: a performance/speech that turns the crowd at the construction kickoff
3. Carson: courage and a rallying voice that gets the other kids in formation
4. Reid: an unbreakable staff that physically blocks the bulldozer
5. Kellen: a puzzle solution that finds the loophole in Driedmore's permit
6. Keira: she's the only one who notices Drystone's secret extra clipboard
7. Ella: she stands face-to-face with Drystone and says no in a way he can't ignore
8. Aiden: he comes back with the unicorn. The unicorn is real. Drystone cannot file a permit on a unicorn.

## Recurring symbols and motifs

- **The C-130** rumbling through the canyon. Portal trigger AND climax timing.
- **Brisket smoke** drifts between worlds.
- **Sparklers** at the end - each kid's, sparkler-lit, after the win.
- **Madison's cookies** travel with them and smell magical to creatures in The Old Roar.
- **The unicorn raft** = Aiden's signature.
- **Screw-Your-Neighbor** as a metaphor for the chosen-vs-blood family question (the game where you swap with your neighbor and trust pays off).

## Voice, tone, and reading level

- **Reading level:** 3rd/4th grade prose. Short sentences. Accessible vocabulary. Action and dialogue forward, not internal monologue heavy. Read-aloud friendly for Aiden, still engaging for Madison.
- **Tone:** Super fun. Light and adventurous. Real stakes (Sun Lakes is at risk), but the threat is fun-beatable, not scary.
- **Humor:** Kid humor. Silly, physical, wordplay. Not parent-humor pretending to be kid-humor.
- **Energy reference:** Narnia x The Penderwicks x FernGully (environmental developer villain).

## Hard rules

1. **No kid is shown a weakness that would make them feel bad about themselves.** Each kid's branch is a CELEBRATION of their strength, not a test of their flaw. The plot needs all eight of them because each one is great at their thing. The developer loses because he doesn't know these kids exist.

2. **No named adults in the story.** Eight kids only. Adults exist offstage (someone made the brisket, someone drove the boat) but they never have a name or a line.

3. **Eight hero branches** (one per kid), not six.

4. **The inflatables room (sunroom) is the portal.** Snake Island is NOT in the book. Forgotten.

5. **No attacking, no humiliation, no exposing.** Conflict is external. The developer attacks Sun Lakes, the kids defend it. No kid is "tested" in a way that puts them down.

6. **Each branch ends in earning ONE THING** that becomes essential in the showdown.
