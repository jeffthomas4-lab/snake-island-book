# Chapter Divisions for Bedtime Reading

This maps the v3 passages into chapters sized for a parent reading aloud to a 6-to-10-year-old. Most chapters land in the 1,800-2,500 word range — about 12-18 minutes aloud, which is the sustainable nightly bedtime window.

The book has natural reading rhythms: framing chapter (one night), each region (one night each), the showdown (split into two nights because it's longer), the coda (one night, or read attached to showdown night 2).

A complete single playthrough = **6 to 9 nights of bedtime reading**, depending on strategy. Replay invites another 6 to 9 nights with different choices.

---

## Chapter 1 — Arrival at Sun Lakes

**Passages:** P-001, P-002, P-003, P-004, P-005
**Word count:** ~2,150
**Read time:** ~14 minutes
**Beats:** Synchronized jump → Drystone arrives → kids can't fix it → sunroom drifts → C-130 portal

End-of-chapter cue: The walls of the sunroom go away. The kids are pulled into The Old Roar. Read this chapter on Night 1 — sets up everything.

---

## Chapter 2 — The Old Roar

**Passages:** P-010, P-011, P-012, then directly into the chosen STRATEGY transition
**Word count:** ~1,500
**Read time:** ~10 minutes
**Beats:** Rafts land → Dry Falls is wet → Harbor Keeper introduces himself → strategy choice → group decides

End-of-chapter cue: The kids choose their strategy and begin to scatter (or stay together). Good night to pause and ask the child reader "what do YOU think they should do?" — turning the strategy choice into a real family decision the next night.

## Chapter 4 — The Showdown, Part 1

**Passages:** P-500, P-501, P-502, Beats 1-4 (Madison, Chayten, Carson, Reid), Drystone pushback (P-550, P-551, P-552)
**Word count:** ~2,600
**Read time:** ~17 minutes
**Beats:** Return to sunroom → walk to dock → Drystone mid-speech → first four kid beats → Drystone plays the bank ace → chosen-family pivot → Carson takes it apart

End-of-chapter cue: Drystone's smile dies. The kids' parents step closer together. Read this chapter on its own night. End on the chosen-family payoff so the next night opens fresh.

## Chapter 6 — Coda

**Passages:** P-600 (count) → P-601 OR P-602
**Word count:** ~500
**Read time:** ~4 minutes
**Beats:** Sparklers, snap-its, dock, the synchronized jump again at sunset, Aiden sees the Harbor Keeper on the far shore

Short closing chapter. Can be read attached to Chapter 5 if the child is still awake and wants the ending, or saved as a separate quiet bedtime moment the night after.

## Total nights of reading

**Minimum playthrough (Strategy A):**
- 1 night: Chapter 1 (arrival)
- 1 night: Chapter 2 (Old Roar + strategy)
- 4 nights: 4 regions (~10-12 min each)
- 1 night: Chapter 4 (Showdown Part 1)
- 1 night: Chapter 5 (Showdown Part 2)
- 1 night: Chapter 6 (Coda)
- **Total: ~9 nights**

**Full playthrough (Strategy B or C):**
- 1 night: Chapter 1
- 1 night: Chapter 2
- 8 nights: 8 regions
- 1 night: Chapter 4
- 1 night: Chapter 5
- 1 night: Chapter 6
- **Total: ~13 nights**

For a one-week vacation, the minimum playthrough fits cleanly — one chapter per night, finishing the book on the last night.

For replay, swap in different paths within the regions and different strategy. The book yields a different feel each time.

## Print layout note

When this goes to Atticus / KDP, each chapter starts on a fresh recto (right-hand) page with a clean title and a chapter number. Chapter headings should NOT include passage IDs (those are internal). Use the chapter titles above:
- *Chapter 1 — Arrival at Sun Lakes*
- *Chapter 2 — The Old Roar*
- *Chapter 3a — Madison at the Quiet Pool*
- etc.

Inside each chapter, passage numbers function as flip-to cross-references. Display them as small labels in the margin or as section breaks — but the chapter heading is what the parent and child see when they open to a new night.
