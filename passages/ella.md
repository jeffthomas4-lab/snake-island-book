## P-170, ARRIVAL AT THE BRIGHT WATER COURT

*~350 words. Slight variant per wrapper.*

Ella's raft slides up onto a moss circle. The raft is now a small wooden boat with golden trim and a velvet seat. The velvet is dark green and the gold is real gold. She steps off, very carefully, because the gold trim catches the sun.

A frog in a tiny crown bows to her.

"Princess."

Ella says, "Princess?"

The frog says, "Your boat has GOLDEN TRIM. Only royalty have golden trim. We've been EXPECTING you."

Ella looks at her boat. It really does have golden trim.

[VARIANT: STAY-TOGETHER. *The other seven kids are standing on the edge of the moss circle, trying not to step on it. Aiden is talking to a frog. Madison is watching the court with her oldest-kid eyes. Carson is suppressing a grin because the frog has just bowed to Ella with extreme formality. Reid bows back to a different frog. The frog bows back to Reid. They are now in a formal exchange that neither of them knows the rules for.*]

[VARIANT: SPLIT. *Three other kids are with her. Aiden is one, he chose her group because he likes how Ella always knows what to do. Keira is the second; she is already drawing the throne. Reid is the third, and he is being polite to the bowing frog. They give her space. They sense this one is hers.*]

[VARIANT: SOLO. *Just her. She likes it. She likes the silence. At home she is the youngest girl and there is always someone in the next room. Here, for one whole hour, she gets to be the person other people are listening to. She straightens her dress without thinking. She makes her face the deciding face without knowing yet that's what it's called.*]

She follows the frog into the glade. There are more frogs. There's a tiny throne in the middle, made of mossy stone. On the throne sits a girl Ella's age, wearing a crown that is slipping over one eye.

The girl is gripping the arms of the throne. Her knuckles are white.

The frog announces, "Princess Ella, daughter of the Lake-Up-Above, attending Her Majesty Queen Marigold of the Bright Water."

Marigold looks up. She is *very* stressed.

"Hi," she whispers. "I've been queen for one week and I am pretty sure I am BAD at it. Please help."

The frogs erupt into argument. They are arguing about whether the new princess (Ella) should sit on the throne. Each frog has a different opinion. Each frog is LOUD. One frog is yelling at another frog about *protocol.* Two more frogs are yelling about whether the protocol-yelling frog is following protocol.

Marigold whispers, "They argue about EVERYTHING."

Ella looks at the frogs.

Ella has TWO older cousins who do this.

She knows exactly how to handle it.

## P-171, THE NOT-THE-DRESS MOMENT

*~280 words. Always read.*

Marigold scoots over on the throne. There's room for two. Ella sits beside her.

Then Marigold does something Ella doesn't expect.

Marigold takes off her crown.

She holds it out in her cupped hands and looks at it. She is just a frightened girl with stringy hair and a too-big robe. She does not look like a queen. She looks like Ella's friend Maya from the third row of her class, who is usually a little overwhelmed but always tries.

Marigold puts the crown back on. Now she looks like a queen again.

Then she takes Ella's hand and puts it on the crown on her head.

"Feel that?"

Ella feels the crown. It is warm. It is just metal.

"That's all it is," Marigold says. "Metal and shine. It's not what makes the queen. Watch."

She takes the crown off again. She sets it on a frog's head. The frog looks ridiculous.

The frog does not act ridiculous. The frog stands up straighter. The frog clears his throat. The other frogs stop arguing for a second and look at him. He says, in a small uncertain voice, "...we should, order? Maybe?"

It works for about three seconds before the other frogs erupt back into argument.

Marigold takes the crown back. She puts it on her own head.

"See? It almost worked. The crown does *some* of the work. But only some. The rest is whether the person under it *decided.* The frog hadn't decided. Most adults haven't decided. That's why crowns mostly don't work."

She looks at Ella.

"I think *you* decide. That's why I knew you were a princess before you stepped off your boat."

Ella sits up straighter.

"The fox is coming. Now you'll see."

## P-172, ELLA'S CHOICE

*~150 words. The real branch.*

The double-doors of the court (which are really two large lily pads on hinges) slam open.

A fox-creature strides in. He is much bigger than the frogs. He is carrying a scroll. He looks confident.

Behind him, hiding under a fern, is a tiny water-creature, round, soft, frightened, with a clutch of small eggs huddled at her feet. The egg-mother. She is shaking.

Marigold says, "Oh no."

## P-173, PATH A: GIVE ADVICE (BASE)

*~500 words. Yields Crown (base).*

Ella takes the throne next to Marigold.

She whispers advice. Marigold listens. The fox unrolls his scroll and starts reading territorial claims in a smooth confident voice. Marigold counters him, using Ella's words, and the fox's claims wobble. The court frogs jump back and forth, taking notes, occasionally yelling about a misplaced clause.

Ella does well. She's smart in court. She knows when to push and when to soften. She helps Marigold get through the case.

In the end, the fox loses his bid (mostly because Ella spotted a forged seal at the bottom of his scroll) and bows out grumpily. The egg-mother is safe. The frogs cheer. Marigold thanks Ella with both hands clasped.

Marigold takes off the crown and produces another from her pocket. "It's our court tradition. The queen always carries two, one to give, when the right person comes. You are the right person."

She places the crown on Ella's head. It fits perfectly. The crown becomes invisible the moment it's settled, only Ella can see it (and Marigold, and anyone who deserves to).

Marigold says, "Now you're a Queen of the Bright Water too. If you ever need us, call my name. We will hear it across worlds."

Ella curtseys. Marigold curtseys back. The frogs erupt in tiny ceremonious clapping.

[CROSSOVER HOOK: *If the reader has played Carson's branch, a small mammoth baby's trumpet carries faintly from somewhere across the plains. The smallest frog says, "Was that, Tuft?" The other frogs argue about whether it was Tuft or Tumble. They argue about this for the rest of the day.*]

Ella walks back to her boat. She is wearing an invisible crown that she alone can see. It feels exactly right on her head.

**→ Exit to P-176.**

## P-174, PATH B: STEP BETWEEN THE FOX AND THE FERN (ENHANCED)

*~600 words. Yields Crown (enhanced) + frog herald ally + egg-mother chorus ally.*

Ella stands up.

She walks down the throne steps. She steps between the fox and the fern.

She is ten years old. She is four feet tall. The fox is twice her size.

The fox tilts his head and smiles a smooth fox smile. "Move along, little princess. The grown-ups are talking."

Ella does not move.

She says, in a voice that is small but is also *exact:* "You will NOT take her home."

Three things happen at once.

The water in the moss circle goes flat. Not still, *flat,* like a lid was pressed onto it from above.

The frogs in the court, every last one, even the loudest, even the protocol-yelling ones, stop arguing. They all turn at the same time and face Ella. They are no longer her audience. They are now her *court.*

And the egg-mother under the fern, who has been afraid all morning, takes the smallest deep breath she has taken in days. It is the breath of a creature who has just heard somebody, *finally,* say the thing she could not say for herself.

The fox blinks. He starts to laugh. The laugh stops in his throat because Ella is *not blinking back* and because everything in the room has just decided to be on her side.

Behind her, Ella hears the smallest sound from the egg-mother, a tiny relieved breath.

The fox tries again. "Princess, the scroll is LEGAL. It is signed. It is BINDING."

Ella says, "Show me where it's signed."

The fox shows her. He shows her the bottom of the scroll. The signature is florid and large.

Ella looks at it. She has a six-year-old cousin who forges his name on permission slips and she knows exactly what forged ink looks like, the way the pen lingers at the start of the curve, the way the loops are too perfect. This scroll has been forged.

She says, loudly enough for the court to hear: "This is a fake. The signature was added later. Look at the way the ink sits ON TOP of the seal instead of UNDER it. He drew it after the seal was already pressed."

The frogs lean in. They all look. They all see it now.

The fox starts to back up.

Ella says, still not blinking, "You will leave. You will not come back. You will not bother the egg-mother again. If you do, the Bright Water Court will know, and I will know, and we will FIND you."

The fox bows. He drops the scroll. He leaves so fast his tail bumps a frog in the head.

The frogs cheer.

The egg-mother comes out from under the fern. She is much smaller than the fox made her seem, a creature about the size of Ella's two palms together, with kind soft eyes and a chorus of tiny round eggs clinging to her back. She bows so low her forehead touches the moss.

"Princess. Thank you."

Ella kneels (which is hard in golden boat-trim shoes but she manages it) and says, "You can come home. Nobody else is taking it. I promise."

The egg-mother says, very softly, "If you ever need us. Anywhere. The whole egg-chorus and I will sing for you. We sing one note all at once. It is, it is quite something. People stop and listen."

Marigold has been silent on the throne, watching all of this. Now she stands.

"Princess Ella. You have just protected the Bright Water. You are no longer a guest in this court. You are a Queen of the Bright Water." She steps down. She places a second crown on Ella's head. "And the egg-mother and her chorus go with you. As does my royal herald. The full strength of the court is yours."

The frog herald (a small dignified frog in a sash) bows.

[CROSSOVER HOOK: *A long ribbon of color drifts across the sky above the court, blue, orange, green, that hadn't been there before. The egg-mother looks up. "The painter girl. She's been at the cliff. The basalt is waking up. We have not seen the basalt wake up in a hundred years."*]

Ella walks back toward her boat. The herald hops along beside her. The egg-mother (and twelve tiny rolling eggs) follow.

**→ Exit to P-176.**

## P-175, PATH C: CO-RULE (HIDDEN)

*~600 words. Yields Crown (full) + Marigold's herald + Bright Water Alliance flag.*

Before the fox can finish his bow, Ella turns to Marigold.

"You don't have to do this alone."

Marigold looks at her sideways.

Ella says, "I'll be queen WITH you. Both of us. Two queens. The fox can't talk circles around two of us at once."

Marigold blinks. Her crown slides a little further over her eye.

"...Can you DO that?" she whispers.

Ella shrugs. "Why not. It's your court. You make the rule."

Marigold sits up straight for the first time all day.

She looks at the court. She looks at the herald. She announces, in a voice she has not used before: "*Court of the Bright Water. From this hour forward, Princess Ella is named Co-Queen. We rule together.*"

The frogs go absolutely silent.

Then they erupt in argument. Six frogs argue about whether co-rule is in the precedents. Three frogs argue about whether the announcement was properly worded. One frog faints from excitement.

The fox tries to interrupt.

Both queens turn to him at once.

The fox stops talking.

Marigold and Ella handle the case together. They sit on the throne side by side, there's barely room, and they tag-team the fox. Marigold knows the law. Ella knows the lying. The fox crumples under both at once. He bows out faster than he came in.

The egg-mother is safe. The court is calm. The frogs are still arguing but they're now arguing about *whether* to applaud, which is at least an upgrade.

Marigold turns to Ella. She is smiling like she hasn't smiled in a week.

"That was the best court session in the history of the Bright Water."

She reaches under the throne and pulls out a small wooden box. Inside is the second crown. She places it on Ella's head and Ella places her own (she'd been wearing a small Sun Lakes hair ornament) onto Marigold's. They've traded.

Marigold stands.

"I declare the Bright Water Court allied to Hotel Sun Lakes. Forever. Any threat to the cove is a threat to us. We will send our herald immediately if a man with clipboards ever sets foot on that dock."

She turns to the frog herald. "*Go. To the lake-up-above. Bring the alliance proclamation. Stand with Co-Queen Ella when she needs you.*"

The frog herald produces a tiny scroll, bows three times, and hops off into a small pool that turns out to lead, somehow, to the world above.

Marigold gives Ella the formal queen-greeting (a kiss on each cheek). The frogs do their best to cheer. The smallest frog faints again.

[CROSSOVER HOOK: *If the reader has played Aiden's branch, a small bright shape passes through the glade for a moment, a unicorn with a small boy on her back. Marigold sees it. "He's a friend of yours?" she asks. "He looks like he believes in things."*]

Ella walks back to her boat. The crown on her head is invisible to most. The egg-mother and her chorus, somehow, have also decided to come.

**→ Exit to P-176.**

## P-176, EXIT

*~200 words. Always read.*

The boat with the gold trim is waiting in the rushes.

[VARIANT: PATH A. *Marigold waves from the throne. The frogs argue about the correct way to wave. Ella laughs.*]

[VARIANT: PATH B. *The herald hops alongside. The egg-mother rides on Ella's shoulder, tiny eggs cradled in a fold of velvet she has commandeered.*]

[VARIANT: PATH C. *The herald is already gone, somewhere ahead, in the world above. Marigold stands and waves from the throne with both hands. She is wearing Ella's Sun Lakes hair ornament. It looks ridiculous on a frog-queen and Marigold loves it.*]

She has the Crown (in whatever form). She has, on her head, a weight that feels less like decoration and more like *deciding.*

She practices it as she pushes off, the deciding face. She tries it once in the reflection of the water. There it is. She did not have it this morning. She has it now.

She is ten years old. She knows what her face is.

That's the line she'll remember. *She knows what her face is.* No one can take that from her now, and nothing she meets at the dock-up-above will be bigger than the new thing she just learned about herself.

She paddles toward the sound of the C-130, faint and far, somewhere up in the world above.

**→ Region complete.**

## Editor notes

**On "the not-the-dress moment" (P-171):** Ella's cared-for beat. Marigold tells her straight: *I knew you were a princess before you stepped off. It wasn't the dress. It was your face. Royalty is a face that decides.* This is the editor's-note fix, Ella is respected for her courage, not for the trappings. The princess aesthetics are not erased; they're contextualized.

**On the deciding-face theme:** Marigold's phrase "the deciding kind" becomes the through-line for Ella at the showdown. When she stands in front of Drystone, what stops him isn't the crown. It's the face. The crown is just so other people know.

**On Path B (fox confrontation):** This is the punch path. Ella catches the forged signature with the *six-year-old cousin who forges permission slips* line, a real-life kid moment, very specific, very age-appropriate. The fox doesn't lose because Ella out-magics him. He loses because she sees what he did and names it.

**On Path C (co-rule):** The thematic punch. Ella's solution to court chaos isn't to take the throne. It's to *share* it. This is the chosen-family theme again, *we rule together, we are stronger because we are not alone.* Marigold trades crowns with her, which is small and warm and exactly the kind of girl-friendship beat that lands for a 9-year-old reader.

**On allies:** Path B yields the egg-mother chorus (visible only to kids and Drystone per locked rule, but their humming is audible to everyone). Path C yields a formal Bright Water Alliance, the herald delivers an actual proclamation at the showdown that declares Hotel Sun Lakes Protected Territory. Drystone can't legally argue with a frog-court alliance but he also can't admit it exists.

**On crossovers:** Path A: Carson's mammoth baby (Tuft/Tumble argument, a tiny scene-stealer). Path B: Madison's Canyon Singer. Path C: Aiden's Pegasus. Same crossover-rotation pattern.
