## P-110, ARRIVAL AT THE QUIET POOL

*~350 words. Slight variant per wrapper (group of 8 / group of 4 / solo).*

Madison's raft slides to a stop on the shore of a pool so still you could roll a coin across it.

The raft is a wooden canoe now, with her name carved into the side: **MADISON**. The tin of cookies she was holding in the sunroom is still in her lap. None of them are crushed. (She checks.)

The pool is shaped like a heart, if a heart had been drawn by someone who'd never seen one. Rocks at the edge. Cottonwoods leaning over the water. A heron standing on one leg in the shallows, looking at her like she's late.

[VARIANT: STAY-TOGETHER. *The other seven kids are climbing out of their rafts behind her. Reid is already studying the rocks. Aiden is whispering something to a frog. Carson is sizing up the heron like he wants to race it. Madison turns and counts heads, eight. All present. Good.*]

[VARIANT: SPLIT. *Three other kids are climbing out behind her, the group she came in with. They give her space. They know this one is hers.*]

[VARIANT: SOLO. *She's alone. The other seven landed somewhere else. The silence is bigger than she expected. She steadies herself. Oldest kid. She knows what she's doing.*]

Three heads pop up out of the water.

Three girls. One her age. One younger. One small.

Their hair drips upward instead of down.

The oldest one tilts her head. "Are you the cookie-bringer?"

The middle one says, "She IS the cookie-bringer."

The youngest one is already chewing on something.

The oldest climbs out. Her feet leave wet prints that turn into flowers. "I'm Pip. That one's Lark. The one chewing is Wren. Wren, what are you eating."

Wren opens both hands. They're full of pondweed she's mashed into the shape of a cookie. It is the saddest cookie Madison has ever seen.

"It's my OWN recipe," Wren says.

"It's TERRIBLE," Pip says.

Madison opens her tin. The cookies inside smell like brown sugar and brisket smoke and home.

She is about to share them.

Then Lark says: "Wait. First, sit down."

## P-111, THE CARED-FOR MOMENT

*~300 words. Always read. This is the fix for the previous editor's note about Madison needing one moment of being taken care of, not just being useful.*

"Wait. Don't sit. STAND. We have been watching you walk down the bank."

Madison blinks. "Why?"

Pip steps closer. "You're a DANCER. We can tell from how you walk. The way your feet land. The way your spine moves with your head. We have been HOPING for a dancer for a very long time."

Lark says, "We knew one of you would be. Pools know. We have been WAITING."

Madison is a little surprised. She knows she's a good dancer, her teacher has told her, her family has told her, she has the trophies and the placements in her room that say so. But she has never met an audience this hungry for a dancer. The Sisters are looking at her the way people look at something they've been hoping to see for years.

Wren tugs her hand. "Can we see you do something? Please? We've been thinking about it for so long."

Pip raises one small wet hand. Three flat stones lift from the pool and hover an inch above the surface, forming a small triangular stage just within Madison's reach. The water around them goes glassy.

Madison thinks for one second. Then she picks the adagio, the one her teacher built FOR her last semester. The hardest one she does in studio. The long arabesque. The turn that took her three months to clean.

She steps onto the first stone. Her body knows what to do.

She dances three measures. They are very good measures. The arabesque is exactly as long as it should be. The turn lands clean. She has danced this same sequence a hundred times. She has never danced it with this much attention from an audience.

The three Sisters do not clap. They do not whistle. They WATCH the way an audience that has been waiting for years watches when the wait is finally over, with their full faces, with their breath held, with the quiet that says *please don't let this end*.

When she steps off the last stone, the pool ripples in a pattern around the three stones that looks, faintly, like a standing ovation made of water.

Pip whispers: "Thank you, Madison. That was BEAUTIFUL."

Lark wipes a Sister-tear that goes up. "We don't get dancers. People don't dance for a pool. They dance for other people. You danced for us anyway."

Madison feels something specific in her chest. Not surprise, she knew she could do it. Not the warmth of being praised, she gets praised, that's familiar. Something newer than that: the warmth of having SHARED something she loves with an audience that genuinely, specifically needed to see it. Her grace went somewhere today that it has never gone before. Three small water-girls had been waiting for years for it. She brought it.

She sits down on the bank. Her hair drips upward, just a little. She catches sight of herself in the pool's reflection, fifteen years old, breath steady, all three Sisters still beaming at her, and laughs out loud. The laugh of someone who brought a gift and discovered the gift was exactly the right one.

Pip puts a small wet hand on Madison's knee. "And one more thing, before you go. You are Madison the Dancer, who shares her grace with audiences who deserve to see it. The pool will remember that name. You should too."

Pip nods. "Go back to your cousins now. We will remember today for a very long time. You were exactly what we had been hoping for."

Madison laughs. It's a real laugh. The kind she usually saves for inside her own head.

The Sisters wait. They've done their part. The rest is hers.

Pip says, "We have something to tell you. About the lake-up-above. About the man with the clipboards."

Madison sits forward.

She is, in fact, ready now.

## P-112, MADISON'S CHOICE

*~150 words. The real branch.*

Pip tells her the rest fast.

The Lake Sisters have been watching the lake-up-above for years. They've seen Mr. Drystone's boats. They've seen him drop papers into the water, papers they were never meant to read. The papers sank. The Sisters caught them. They have them now.

But the papers are in three pieces, and only one piece is here at the pool. The other two are elsewhere.

Pip is holding the first piece. It glints in her hand.

"How do you want to do this?" Pip asks.

Madison thinks for a second.

> **If you take the piece Pip has and head back to the others, turn to P-113.** *(The fast path. You'll have a Map. Not the whole map, but enough.)*

## P-113, PATH A: SHARE THE COOKIES, TAKE THE PIECE (BASE)

*~600 words. Yields Map (base version). No ally.*

Madison opens her tin and shares the cookies fairly. One for Pip. One for Lark. The biggest one for Wren, because Wren is smallest.

(Wren cries with joy. Lark and Pip do not comment. They've seen Wren cry over a particularly good rock.)

The Sisters eat their cookies the way creatures eat who have never had anything baked before. Pip nibbles. Lark inhales hers in two bites. Wren tries to make her cookie last by licking it.

When they're done, Pip looks at her sisters. The three of them nod at each other in the way of people who have just received something and now have to give something back. (That's how exchanges work in the Old Roar. You bring something. They bring something. That is the whole agreement.)

Lark slips back into the pool and comes up holding a small, perfectly round, lavender-colored stone. It is warm in her hand. It is glowing very faintly, the way a smooth river rock looks when a kid pulls it out of shallow water and holds it up to the sun.

"For you," she says. "It's a *quiet stone.* Hold it when a place is too loud. It pulls the noise into itself and gives you back about ten seconds of quiet. Sisters made it. It only works if you got it from a sister."

Madison closes her hand around it. It is exactly the kind of thing she did not know she needed and has needed her whole life.

Wren hands her something next. It is a small pondweed-and-pebble bracelet she has clearly been making during the cookie sharing. It is, like Wren's cookie, the saddest bracelet ever assembled.

"I MADE it," Wren says, very proud.

"It's beautiful," Madison says. And means it.

(She slips it on her wrist. It tickles. She does not take it off.)

Then Pip hands Madison the paper.

It is a single sheet, water-warped at the edges, the ink swimming a little but readable. It shows the cove. It shows the data center building Drystone plans to put on the dry lakebed once the water is gone. It shows the drainage path, straight down the canyon, into the river system, away forever.

It does NOT show the bank fraud. It does NOT show the lowballed appraisal. Those pieces are elsewhere.

Madison rolls the paper carefully and tucks it inside her cookie tin. The tin clicks closed. It is warm in her hands.

"This is most of it," Pip says. "Not all. But you'll know what to say."

Madison nods.

[CROSSOVER HOOK: *If the reader has already played Aiden's branch and unlocked Pegasus, a small rainbow arcs over the pool for no reason. Lark looks up. "Huh," she says. "That hasn't happened before." Madison doesn't know what it means yet. She will.*]

Wren tugs her hand. "Will you come back?"

Madison kneels so she's eye-level with the smallest Sister. Wren's eyes are the color of pool water in the morning.

"Yes," Madison says.

She means it.

The Sisters walk her back toward the meadow. Pip stops at the edge of the trees. "Tell our older sister hi."

Madison says, "Who's your older sister?"

Lark says, "The big one. The one your hotel is named for. The cove."

Madison stops walking.

The lake at home. *Their* lake. The one she's been swimming in every July of her life. The one her cousins all swim in. The one Aiden took his first dip in last summer with his life jacket on backwards.

That lake is family to these girls.

That lake is family to HER.

She feels something land in her chest that she's been almost-knowing for years.

She walks back to where the others are waiting (or back to her canoe, if solo) with the Map in her tin and a feeling in her chest she'll figure out later.

## P-114, PATH B: ASK WHAT THEY NEED FIRST (ENHANCED)

*~700 words. Yields Map (enhanced) + Branch the beaver as ally.*

Madison doesn't open the tin yet.

She looks at Pip and says, "What do you NEED?"

Pip blinks. Lark blinks. Wren stops chewing.

"Most people," Pip says slowly, "don't ask us that."

Lark adds, "Most people are HUNGRY for what we have. They don't notice we might need something too."

Madison waits.

The Sisters look at each other in the way sisters look at each other.

Pip says, "The other two pieces of the map. We can't reach them."

The first piece, the one Pip is holding, sank near the pool, easy to catch.

The other two sank farther downstream. Out past where the pool turns into a slow river. Out where a grumpy old beaver named **Branch** has been collecting interesting things in his lodge for two hundred years. Branch caught the other two pieces and he will not give them back. He says the river is his and what lands in the river is his.

The Sisters have asked him a hundred times. He always says no.

"He might say yes to YOU," Pip says. "He likes people who SIT."

Madison thinks about her own grandmother. Her grandmother also liked people who sit.

Madison says, "Take me to him."

The Sisters slip into the water. Madison walks along the bank. Lark pops up every few steps to point. The pool narrows. The water starts moving. Cottonwood leaves spin past on the current.

At a bend in the river, a beaver lodge the size of a small cabin rises out of the water. Smoke comes out of a hole in the top. The smoke smells like dried mint.

Madison knocks on a stick.

A grumpy face appears. **Branch**. He has a small pair of spectacles on the end of his nose and a tea towel over one shoulder.

"What."

Madison says, "Hello, Branch. I'm Madison. May I come in?"

Branch is suspicious. But Madison didn't lead with what she wanted. Branch finds this confusing in a way he likes.

"...Fine."

Inside the lodge: a tiny rocking chair, a tea kettle on a stone fire, and a wall of WONDERFUL trash. A spoon. A fishing lure. A pair of sunglasses. A small ceramic pig. Two folded pieces of paper that Madison's eyes go to immediately and then, very deliberately, away from.

Branch pours her a thimble of tea. (For a beaver, a thimble is a normal-sized cup. For Madison, it is approximately one drop.)

She sits.

She sips her one drop of tea.

She does not ask about the papers.

She asks about the spoon.

Branch lights up. He tells her about the spoon. The spoon takes nine minutes. Then he tells her about the ceramic pig. The pig takes longer.

Forty minutes go by. (Time in The Old Roar is slower, but still, forty minutes is forty minutes. The Harbor Keeper said *time for four*. She's spending it.)

At the end of his stories, Branch is quiet for a long time. Then he says, "You're the first person in two hundred years who didn't ask me for the papers first."

Madison says, "I figured you'd tell me about them when you wanted to."

Branch hands her the two folded papers without ceremony.

Then he says, "I'm coming with you."

Madison says, "To the lake-up-above?"

"To wherever the man with the clipboards is," Branch says. "I'll witness."

She rolls the three pieces of the Map together. Now it shows everything: the drainage, the data center, the bank fraud, the lowballed appraisal, the wrong permit Drystone filed. The whole shape of the lie.

Branch waddles to the door, picks up a small bow tie from his wall of trash, and clips it on.

"Ready," he says.

## P-115, PATH C: NOTICE WREN FIRST (HIDDEN)

*~700 words. Yields Map (full) + CanyonSinger flag. Hardest to find. Most rewarding.*

Madison was about to open her tin.

She doesn't.

Because Wren has gone quiet.

The smallest Sister was chewing her pondweed cookie a second ago. Now she's holding it in both hands and staring at the pool.

Madison kneels. "Wren. Hey. What's the matter?"

Pip and Lark go still.

Wren says, in a voice so small Madison has to lean in: "She didn't sing last night."

Madison says, "Who didn't?"

Pip says, very quickly, "Nobody. It's nothing."

Lark says, "Pip."

Pip closes her mouth.

Wren says, "The lady. The one who sings."

Madison sits down on the bank. The wood is warm. "Tell me."

The Sisters look at each other. Then Pip nods.

Lark sings, very quietly, three notes. The pool hums them back. The three notes are beautiful, clear, full, the kind of voice that carries.

"That's HER voice," Wren says. "She sings here some nights. Not all. Some. She's been singing here for as long as we've been here. We don't know who she is. The lake remembers her. We listen for her."

Madison's skin prickles.

Because Madison knows that voice.

She doesn't know HOW she knows it. She just does. Like the way she knows the smell of brisket smoke. Like the way she knows the sound of the C-130 a full second before it shows up over the canyon.

She HAS heard this voice. Two summers ago. A hot July night, late, after the brisket was gone and the kids were supposed to be asleep. The sound came across the water from somewhere nobody could place, somebody, somewhere, singing opera. Big, beautiful, impossible. The grown-ups had laughed about it the next morning. They'd all pretended not to know who it was. The mystery had become a family joke.

But Madison had watched her mother's face that next morning. Her mother had been very busy looking at her coffee.

Madison feels her chest do a thing.

She doesn't say anything yet.

She says: "Why didn't she sing last night?"

Pip says, "We don't know. She comes when she comes. When she doesn't, the lake is quieter than it should be. Wren feels it most."

Wren is crying without making a sound. The tears go up her face instead of down.

Madison does the thing she does best in the whole world.

She puts an arm around the smallest Sister. She doesn't say *it'll be okay*. She doesn't say *don't worry*. She just holds her and lets her cry the wrong direction.

After a while, Pip says quietly, "There's another piece of the map."

Madison says, "I thought there were three."

Pip says, "There are. But there's also a fourth. It's not on paper. It's a song. The song the lady sings. If you can hear it, you'll know what the lake itself thinks about Mr. Drystone. The lake has an opinion."

Lark says, "It's not loud. But it's real."

Madison thinks for a second.

Then she does the only thing she can do, the thing she's actually good at.

Pip seems to know. She lifts a hand. Three flat stones rise up out of the pool, one after another, arranged in a line across the water. They are perfectly still. They are also, Madison notices, the same color as the basalt at home.

Madison steps onto the first one. It holds.

She knows the count before her head does. She has danced this same sequence a hundred times this spring; her body falls into it the way an old book falls open at the right page.

She steps to the second stone in one clean line, the way her old teacher used to drill it: head up, shoulders down, the foot finding the place before you trust it. She turns once on the second stone. She lands.

She crosses to the third stone and finishes in fourth position, arms soft, eyes closed.

She holds it.

The pool hums.

Three notes. Low. Then a long pause.

And then, from somewhere far away, maybe under the pool, maybe somewhere on the other side of the canyon, three notes come back. Sung in answer. The voice is a woman's. Operatic. Big. Beautiful. She is singing in The Old Roar tonight after all. She is answering Madison's dance the way a singer answers a question.

Wren laughs through her wrong-direction tears.

Pip presses both hands to her mouth.

Lark just listens.

The lady's song goes on for a long minute. None of them speak. Madison's eyes are wet too, though she couldn't tell you why.

When the song ends, Pip hands Madison the first piece of the map. Then she dives into the pool and comes up holding the second piece, then the third. They were here all along. The Sisters had been waiting to see if she was the right person to give them to.

She is.

The three pieces fit together cleanly. The whole Map. Everything Drystone has done, everything he plans.

"And the song?" Madison asks.

Pip says, "You'll carry it. The lake will remember that you heard."

Madison rolls the Map. She tucks it in her tin.

The tin glows, very faintly, like firelight inside a paper lantern.

She doesn't know what she's going to tell her mom about this later.

She'll figure it out.

## P-116, EXIT: THE SISTERS WAVE HER OFF

*~250 words. Always read. Slight variant per path.*

Madison walks back toward the meadow. (Or toward her canoe, if solo. Or toward her group, if split.)

The Sisters wave from the water. Their hair drips upward. Wren waves with both hands and a half-eaten pondweed cookie.

Pip calls after her: "Tell our older sister hi."

Madison stops.

She turns back. "Who's your older sister?"

Lark says, "The big one. The one your hotel is named for. The cove."

[VARIANT: PATH A. *Madison feels something land in her chest she'll figure out later.*]

[VARIANT: PATH B. *Madison feels something land in her chest. Behind her, Branch the beaver in a small bow tie says, "Tell her I said hi too." Madison promises she will.*]

[VARIANT: PATH C. *Madison feels something land in her chest. She also feels, somewhere far below the pool, a woman's voice humming the start of a song. The lake is family. The song is family. She'll figure the rest out later.*]

Her canoe is waiting.

She steps in.

She has the Map (in whatever form she earned it). She has, somewhere in her bones, the start of a feeling about chosen family that she's going to need at the dock in about an hour.

The canoe pushes off. The Sisters wave from the water.

Madison heads back toward the sound of the C-130, faint and far, somewhere up in the world above.
