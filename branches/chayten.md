# Chapter 3 - Chayten's Branch (13)

~2,000 words / 15-min read for a 3rd grader.

Lead trait: connector, paddle-boards with Madison and fishes with little brother Aiden, performance, persuasion, fierce protector. Heart of gold.

## What she earns
**The Voice.** A magical voice that, when she speaks at the showdown, makes everyone stop and listen. Plus a stage trick: she knows how to make a crowd ROAR for her.

## The task (her prize quest)
Step in for the lead role in the Driftwood Players' biggest play of the season. Curtain in one hour.

## Intro (~300 words)

Chayten's raft (now a wooden paddle-board with a velvet seat at the back) slides up to a bank where someone is screaming.

Not a scared scream. A theatrical scream.

Eight otters in tiny costumes are running in circles. One otter is lying on his back with a paw on his forehead.

"My WHISKERS," he wails. "I CANNOT GO ON."

A different otter, in a top hat, sees Chayten step off her raft. He freezes. He stares. He turns to the other otters.

"FRIENDS," he announces. "OUR LEAD HAS ARRIVED."

Chayten says, "Wait, what?"

The top-hat otter is named Otto. He explains in one breath: the Driftwood Players are about to perform "The Sad Saga of the Sturgeon Who Forgot His Lines" for the Audience of the Standing Stones. Their lead actor (the one with the paw on his forehead) sprained his whiskers. Chayten has perfect tragedy eyes. She's the right size. The costume fits her. The play starts in one hour. Will she do it?

Chayten asks how many lines.

Otto says: "Twelve verses."

Chayten asks if she gets to read them in advance.

Otto laughs. "Read? READ? My dear, this is THEATER."

## Decision 1: Which otter to ask for help (~300 words)

The otters all want to coach her. Each gives terrible different advice.

- **A. Ask Otto.** (Otto teaches her grandeur. Big gestures. Big voice. She'll be DRAMATIC.)
- **B. Ask Sienna, the otter who plays the Sturgeon's grandmother.** (Sienna teaches her tenderness. Small voice. The audience leans in. She'll be SUBTLE.)
- **C. Ask Pickle, the otter who paints the sets.** (Pickle teaches her STAGE TRICKS. Where to stand. How to use her props. She'll be SMART about it.)

All three paths lead to her getting on stage. Each gives her a different style for the showdown speech later.

## Decision 2: How to costume

The costume is enormous. It's the Sturgeon's robe, with whiskers and fins.

- **A. Wear the whole thing.** (Looks dramatic. Hard to move. The audience laughs in the right places.)
- **B. Wear just the headpiece.** (Looks elegant. The audience listens harder. Less laughter, more emotion.)
- **C. Modify it.** (Use the costume as a CAPE instead. The otters are scandalized but impressed. She invents a new style.)

## Decision 3: The audience arrives

The Standing Stones are giant rocks shaped like people. They sit in rows and they don't say a word. Audiences in The Old Roar do not applaud with hands. They applaud by HUMMING.

Chayten goes on stage.

The audience hums when she enters. The hum is so loud her bones rattle.

- **A. Hum back.** (The Stones love this. They're paying attention before she even speaks.)
- **B. Bow.** (Classic. They appreciate her respect.)
- **C. Wink.** (Bold. The Stones HUM louder. They're ready.)

## Decision 4: Halfway through, she forgets a line - AND sees Aiden

It's a long play. She forgets the seventh verse. Otto is in the wings panicking. The other otters look at her.

In the half-second of silence, she sees something. Just for a flash. She sees AIDEN. Her little brother. He's somewhere else in The Old Roar - on a unicorn, of all things, riding through air. He's smiling.

The play she's performing is "The Sad Saga of the Sturgeon Who Forgot His Lines" - a play about a young fish whose older friends help him remember who he is. Chayten realizes the play is about Aiden. She's been thinking about him the whole time without knowing it.

She remembers the line. She also adds a new one.

- **A. Make up a line that fits the play AND echoes what Aiden would say.** (Improv with heart. The audience hums tears.)
- **B. Pause and ask the audience.** (One Stone hums the line back to her. She speaks it like she's speaking TO Aiden, not to the audience.)
- **C. Tell the truth - "I forgot the line, but it doesn't matter, my friends will tell me."** (The audience LOVES her honesty. The Standing Stones roar-hum.)

Each path becomes a performance trick she'll use at the showdown. AND it cements what she earned was always for protecting Aiden.

## Decision 5: The final verse

The play is about to end. The Sturgeon either remembers his lines or doesn't. Chayten chooses how the play ends.

- **A. The Sturgeon remembers.** (Triumphant. Audience hums in joy. Otters give her the mask, no ally at showdown.)
- **B. The Sturgeon never remembers but his friends help him through anyway.** (Heart-of-gold ending. **The Driftwood Players show up at the showdown** as a cheering section that drowns out Crumb when he tries to interrupt.)
- **C. The Sturgeon decides he doesn't need lines because his FRIENDS speak for him.** (Chayten's signature ending. Otto sends his TOP HAT with her - it becomes a real hat in the real world. Chayten wears it at the showdown. The hat is a sequel hook.)

## Crescendo close (~300 words)

The play ends. The Standing Stones hum so loud the cliffs vibrate. The otters lift Chayten on their shoulders.

Otto pulls her aside.

"You weren't just acting," he says. "You were SPEAKING. You held that audience because you wanted them to hear you, not because you wanted them to LIKE you. There's a difference."

He hands her a mask. It's small, otter-sized.

"Take this. When you put it on, your voice carries to anyone you want to hear it. A whole crowd. A noisy room. A man who isn't listening."

Chayten asks, "How does it work?"

Otto says, "Truthfully. Just speak truthfully and they'll hear."

He hugs her with otter arms.

The Driftwood Players line up to wave goodbye. Pickle gives her a small fish she sneaked from props ("for your brother, you said he likes fish").

Chayten steps onto her raft. The paddle-board slides backward into the water.

She has the mask in her pocket. She knows her voice can carry now.

She paddles away. Behind her, the otters start rehearsing tomorrow's play. Otto trips on his hat.

## How this feeds the showdown
At the data center groundbreaking, after Madison cracks open his story, Chayten steps up to the makeshift podium. Her WORDS hold the crowd. The mask amplifies but she's the speaker. The reason her voice carries is that it's TRUTHFUL - she's the same kid she was on the back dock with Aiden, just with a bigger audience. She says what's actually true and the crowd has to hear it.

She brings the small fish for Aiden because she's STILL his protective big sister.

If the reader picked Decision 5B, **the Driftwood Players show up at the showdown** as a cheering section that drowns out Crumb every time he tries to interrupt.

## Notes
- The "Sturgeon Who Forgot His Lines" is built specifically for Chayten's path. The play's theme (friends speaking for you) mirrors the chosen family theme.
- The otters are funny because they're small and dramatic and proud. None of them are mocked or made to look bad. They just ARE goofy.
- The mask is the talisman, but the real prize is what Otto teaches her: speak to be heard, not to be liked.
