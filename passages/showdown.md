# The Showdown, Eight Against Drystone (v3)

**Passages:** P-500 through P-602
**Read order:** Always sequential, but each beat has a state-check. Reader (or book layout) picks the version that matches what they earned.

This is the modular CYOA showdown. Each of the 8 kid beats has a MAIN version (requires the item the kid earned) and a FALLBACK (works without it). The fallbacks are written so the kid wins their beat through *who they are,* not through what they earned, no kid ever looks weak because the reader missed an item.

Target ~7,000 words total. Setup ~600. Beats ~700 each (×8). Drystone pushback ~800. Climax + endings ~1,200.

## P-500, RETURN TO THE SUNROOM

*~250 words.*

The kids land in the sunroom one by one. The rafts come to rest. The walls of the sunroom resolve back into walls. The smell of brisket comes through the cracked door. The plastic of the inflatables is warm.

Pegasus walks in last, hooves clicking on the wood floor. She is real. She is also, apparently, taller than the doorway. She ducks. The other seven kids stare. None of them are surprised, exactly. None of them are not surprised, either.

Aiden grins like he's been keeping the best secret in the world.

They show each other what they have.

Madison opens her cookie tin. The map inside is rolled tight. The tin glows faintly.

Chayten pulls the otter mask from her pocket. It is small and warm.

Carson lifts the tusk-charm. It is smooth and humming.

Reid holds the staff. The wood is the color of wet basalt and a low hum runs through it whenever his palm touches the grain.

Kellen pats the stone in his pocket. It is heavier than it looks.

Keira tucks the Truth Brush behind her ear. It glows.

Ella has the crown. Nobody sees it but the kids and (later) Drystone.

Aiden has Pegasus.

[ALLY ROLL CALL: Any earned allies are gathered too. Branch the beaver in the hallway. Otto in a window. Big Coulee outside the back wall. Old Speak as a thicket of new young trees that wasn't there yesterday. Skim and her flock in the yard. The egg-mother and her chorus near the dock. Marigold's frog herald on the porch. Pickle on Chayten's shoulder. Hush on Aiden's shoulder. Cliffy walking slowly toward the lake. *Visible to the kids. Visible, and ONLY visible, to Mr. Drystone when the moment comes.*]

Pegasus says, "Is THIS the lake-up-above?"

The kids look at each other and start laughing.

Outside, the C-130 thunders past for the second time today. The construction kickoff is in fifteen minutes.

They walk down to the dock together.

## P-501, WALK TO THE DOCK

*~200 words.*

Eight kids walk down the gravel path to the dock.

A unicorn walks behind them, invisible to anyone over eighteen.

[CONDITIONAL ALLIES: any earned allies trail behind, visible only to the kids. Big Coulee thumps along beside them at half-speed, even at half-speed she shakes the ground.]

The brisket smell follows them. A C-130 had thundered overhead the night before. Another would do so this afternoon. That is how 4th of July week works at Sun Lakes.

The dock is set up for a construction kickoff. Small platform at the end of the dock. Folding chairs (mostly empty). A bulldozer parked on the dirt road, engine off. A banner stretched between two poles:

> DRIEDMORE DATA CENTER, WEST CASCADIA HUB
> GROUNDBREAKING JULY 4, NOON

A modest crowd has gathered. Some adults from town. A few hotel guests. The kids' parents off to one side, talking quietly. They look worried. They look like adults who have been told there's no other option and have started believing it.

The kids' parents have not seen the kids yet.

Mr. Drystone is at the platform, mid-speech. He has three clipboards balanced on the lectern and a fourth tucked inside his jacket. He keeps glancing at his tablet.

He has not seen the kids either.

That is about to change.

## BEAT 1, MADISON


> **Reader: which version do you read?**

### P-510, MAIN (REQUIRES: Map)

*Madison the Dancer.*

*~400 words.*

Madison opens her cookie tin on the platform railing.

The tin glows. Faintly, but it glows.

She unrolls the map. She lays it flat on the railing so the crowd can see it. The map is water-warped at the edges and the ink shimmers in a way that ink usually doesn't.

She doesn't shout. She doesn't have to.

"Mr. Drystone," she says, "this is a copy of your real plans. Not the public sign. The real ones. They show the water draw rate. The drainage path. The wrong permit you filed."

Drystone stutters. "Where did you GET that."

Madison says, "From a friend who lives in the lake."

Drystone laughs the dismissive laugh of a man whose career is about to end. "From a friend who LIVES in the LAKE. Right."

The crowd half-laughs with him. They're not sure yet.

[CONDITIONAL, IF Branch is an ally: *Branch the beaver waddles up onto the platform. He is wearing a small bow tie. He says, in a creaky beaver voice, "I live in the lake." The crowd goes very quiet. Only the kids and Drystone see Branch clearly. The adults see a... blur. A small dark shape. A very specific small dark shape with what appears to be formalwear. They cannot quite focus.*]

Madison continues. She names the water draw, millions of gallons a day, every day, forever. She names the bank: Driedmore owns it, the appraisal was rigged. She names the wrong permit. She points to each on the map.

[CONDITIONAL, IF Map (full): *Madison's tin is glowing brighter. The full map shows everything, including a small handwritten note in the margin that Drystone did not know was on his own paperwork. Madison reads it out: an internal memo about how to "manage local sentiment." The crowd stops laughing.*]

Some of the parents start listening. They had been told this was the only way. They had not been told it would mean draining the lake.

Drystone is sweating.

> **Continue to Beat 2.**

### P-511, FALLBACK (NO Map)

*~300 words.*

Madison doesn't have the map. She has a tin of cookies.

She opens it. She walks down off the platform. She doesn't say a word.

She offers a cookie to each adult in the crowd. The cookies are chocolate-chip with a small amount of cinnamon she put in last night, when she was sitting at her grandmother's kitchen counter listening to her mother on the phone, talking quietly about "the situation." She heard every word. She has heard every word every night for two weeks.

The adults take the cookies. They eat them. The cookies are warm. The cookies are exactly the kind of cookie a fifteen-year-old who has been paying attention makes when she wants to bring something real to a place she loves.

While they eat, Madison talks.

"My mom has been on the phone every night for two weeks. I've heard the calls. I know what the bank is asking. I know the rate they're offering. I know what we'd lose if we said yes. I'm fifteen years old. I have been listening. I made these cookies last night, in case it helped today."

She gets back on the platform. She looks at Drystone.

"You assumed I wasn't paying attention. None of you grown-ups noticed I was listening. I was. I always am. So when you say my family is broke and Driedmore is the only buyer, I know it isn't true. I heard my mom say so on the phone four nights ago."

The adults are very quiet. Several are still chewing. One, Reid's mother, has tears in her eyes that don't have anything to do with the cookies.

Madison hasn't won the beat with evidence. She has won it by being the one person in the cove who was actually *listening* the whole time.

> **Continue to Beat 2.**

## BEAT 2, CHAYTEN


> **Reader: which version do you read?**
> *If you played Chayten's chapter, read the MAIN version below (Chayten has her otter mask).*

### P-520, MAIN (REQUIRES: Voice)

*Chayten the Connector.*

*~400 words.*

Chayten steps to the edge of the platform. She doesn't take the lectern from Drystone. She doesn't need it.

She slips the otter mask out of her pocket and presses it to her face for one second. Then she tucks it back.

The crowd doesn't see the mask. They feel something change in the air.

Chayten says, "Listen to her."

Three words. The crowd STOPS shifting. Stops fidgeting. Every eye locks on Chayten.

She says, "Listen to all of us. We have something to say. It is the truth and you should hear it."

[CONDITIONAL, IF Otto is an ally: *Otto the otter, bandaged whiskers, velvet cape over one shoulder, pads onto the platform behind her. The adults see... not quite an otter. A small dignified dark shape they cannot quite name. Drystone sees him plainly. Drystone starts to ask "what IS that" and Otto bows formally to him, which makes it worse.*]

[CONDITIONAL, IF Pickle is an ally: *Pickle is perched on Chayten's shoulder, no bigger than a teacup. She hisses at Drystone. Drystone is the only adult who hears it. He flinches.*]

[CONDITIONAL, IF Voice (full) AND Pickle: *One of the Standing Stones, somewhere far below in The Old Roar, hums a single deep note. The dock vibrates. Three glasses on a folding table shatter. A child in the crowd points at the lake and says "Mom did the lake just THRUM?" Nobody can answer.*]

Behind Chayten, in the back of the crowd, Aiden holds the silver fish Pickle gave him. He smiles at his sister. She smiles back. The whole reason her voice carries is that she's standing here for him and for the seven others. The mask just lets her speak loud enough for the crowd to actually hear it.

Madison continues from where she left off. She names the data center plan. She names what it would cost the lake. She names what the family was going to lose.

The crowd is listening.

Drystone tries to interrupt.

The crowd looks at him with a kind of annoyance he hasn't experienced from a crowd before.

He shuts up.

> **Continue to Beat 3.**

### P-521, FALLBACK (NO Voice)

*~300 words.*

Chayten doesn't have the mask. So she does what she does at home when a room won't settle.

She walks to the small group of adults standing nearest the platform. She looks at one of the dads, a man she's known her whole life, who fishes off the back dock with her brother, and she says, very quietly, just to him: "Mr. Halverson. Will you sit down? I want to tell you something."

He sits down. Cross-legged. On the dock. In his work clothes.

She walks to the next adult. "Mrs. Bell. Sit down." Mrs. Bell sits.

She walks the crowd. One at a time. Not asking the crowd to listen. Asking *each person* to sit. They do. Because it's Chayten, and Chayten is the kid who always remembered their dog's name, and the kid who let your toddler tag along on the paddle board last summer, and the kid who you have been seeing for years and have always assumed was good without ever having to wonder.

Within two minutes, every adult in the crowd is sitting on the dock. Cross-legged. Looking up.

Madison delivers her cookie speech (or her map). The crowd cannot fidget because they have committed to sitting. They cannot leave because nobody wants to be the first one to stand up.

Drystone tries to keep speaking from the platform. He looks down at twenty grown adults sitting on a dock listening to a thirteen-year-old. He sounds, suddenly, ridiculous.

He stops talking.

Chayten hasn't won the beat with magic. She has won it by knowing that grown-ups will do almost anything a calm kid asks them to do, if the kid asks them by name, one at a time.

> **Continue to Beat 3.**

### P-530, MAIN (REQUIRES: Charm AND 5+ kids present)

*Carson the Trustworthy.*

*~400 words.*

Drystone has had enough. He says, "This is RIDICULOUS. I'm starting the bulldozer."

He stomps off the platform toward the bulldozer.

Carson steps in front of it.

He doesn't say anything.

Reid lines up beside him. Then Kellen. Then Keira. Then Ella. Then Madison and Chayten and Aiden. Pegasus shoulders in behind Aiden, head down, horn glowing.

Eight kids in a line in front of a bulldozer. (Nine, if you count the unicorn. The adults don't.)

The tusk-charm at Carson's neck glows. The other kids feel it, a warm hum in their own chests, but the line was forming because Carson was already there, not because of the charm. The charm just makes the bond visible.

[CONDITIONAL, IF Big Coulee is an ally: *Big Coulee thunders down the dock road, flower crown still tilted over one eye, narrating her own approach: "AND BIG COULEE arrives, looking STRONG, looking READY, looking like the AUNTIE these KIDS NEED." She skids to a stop behind the kids. The crowd of adults sees... nothing. The dock shakes. Several adults look around for what just made the dock shake. They cannot quite focus on the enormous wooly mammoth standing behind a row of children. Drystone, however, sees her perfectly. He has stopped walking.*]

[CONDITIONAL, IF Tuft AND Tumble are allies: *Two baby mammoths trot forward and sit, one on either side of Carson, their tiny trunks resting on his shoulders.*]

Drystone says, "I am calling the police."

Carson says, his voice steady, "Go ahead. Call them. Show them your real plans."

Drystone starts to dial. The crowd watches. Carson does not move. None of the kids move.

After a moment, Drystone lowers the phone.

He cannot, in fact, call the police on eight children standing in front of a bulldozer.

The dock holds.

### P-531, FALLBACK (NO Charm OR <5 kids)

*~300 words.*

Drystone stomps toward the bulldozer.

Carson is not at the front of the line. He's at the back.

He has, all morning, been standing at the back of the kids, that's where he always stands, behind Aiden, hands ready, scanning. The competitive cousin who wants to win at everything has spent the entire showdown so far as the rear guard. Nobody noticed. That's how good he is at it.

When Drystone makes for the bulldozer, Carson moves up through the line of cousins. He puts his hand on Aiden's shoulder as he passes, a small "I see you" squeeze, then on Ella's, then on Keira's. Then he steps to the front.

He doesn't block the bulldozer. He turns *around* and faces the other seven kids.

"We do this together."

It is not a command. It is a reminder. He has been the cousin who reminds people of things since he was nine years old.

The other seven kids look at him. They look at each other.

They all step forward, together. Not in a line. In a *clump.* Aiden's hand finds Madison's. Reid puts his hand on Ella's shoulder, the way Carson did. Kellen grabs Keira's elbow. Chayten gets behind Aiden, hand on his other shoulder.

By the time Drystone turns from the bulldozer, the kids are a single thing. A tangle. Eight bodies arranged so that none of them could move forward without all of them moving forward.

Drystone cannot drive over a tangle of eight children any more than he can drive over a line of eight.

Carson hasn't rallied the kids with the charm. He has rallied them by being the one who knows how to *gather* without leading.

### P-540, MAIN (REQUIRES: Staff)

*Reid the Noticer.*

*~350 words.*

Drystone climbs into the bulldozer. He starts the engine. It roars.

The kids don't move.

Reid steps forward, calm as he always is, and plants the staff in the dirt directly in front of the bulldozer's tracks.

The staff stands on its own. The wood hums, a low slow note.

[CONDITIONAL, IF Staff (full): *The staff visibly takes root. The wood at its base sends pale tendrils into the soil. The kids can see them. The adults cannot. Drystone can.*]

The bulldozer rolls forward.

It hits the staff.

The staff doesn't move.

The bulldozer's tracks SPIN. Smoke comes off them. The bulldozer doesn't go anywhere.

Drystone revs the engine harder. The tracks dig grooves into the road. The staff stays exactly where it is.

Reid says, calmly, "It's not going to move."

The crowd actually laughs. The bulldozer keeps spinning. Drystone is sweating through his hard hat. After thirty long seconds he gives up and turns off the engine.

[CONDITIONAL, IF Old Speak is an ally: *A thicket of trees that wasn't there an hour ago is now standing at the edge of the dock. The trees are tall and quiet. They lean slightly toward Drystone. The kids' parents look at the trees and try to remember if those trees were always there. They cannot remember. Old Speak, in his slow tree-being way, raises one bark-bristled hand. He waves.*]

Drystone climbs down. His hard hat falls off.

> **Continue to Drystone's pushback.**

### P-541, FALLBACK (NO Staff)

*~300 words.*

Reid doesn't have the staff.

He has a pocket knife and a piece of cottonwood he picked up on the walk down.

While Drystone is climbing into the bulldozer, Reid sits down. Cross-legged. On the dock. Right in front of the bulldozer's tracks. And he starts to whittle.

Drystone stares at him.

Reid doesn't look up.

He whittles. He has been whittling since he was seven. His hands know what to do without his eyes. The cottonwood is soft. The shape inside is already there. He works.

The bulldozer engine starts. Reid does not look up.

The engine revs. Reid does not look up.

The bulldozer rolls forward six inches. The driver, Drystone, sees that the kid is not moving and is not even *looking.* The kid is just whittling, the way you whittle at home on the back porch on a hot afternoon when you've got nothing else to do and nowhere else to be.

You cannot run a bulldozer over a kid who is whittling.

The bulldozer stops.

Reid keeps whittling.

A full minute passes. Then another. The whole crowd watches a fourteen-year-old patiently shaping a piece of cottonwood. He is not performing. He is not trying. He is just *whittling,* with the calm of someone who knows the world will reorganize itself around him if he waits long enough.

Drystone shuts off the engine. The crowd realizes only then that they have all been holding their breath.

Reid looks up for the first time. He holds up what he's carved. It is a small wooden fish. He hands it to Aiden, who has been watching from the side.

"Here. For your pole."

Reid hasn't won the beat with the staff. He has won it by being exactly the kid he always is, with exactly the calm he always has, in front of a man who has never met anyone like that in his life.

## P-550, CRUMB'S PUSHBACK: THE BANK OFFER

*~300 words.*

Drystone climbs out of the bulldozer. He's furious. He grabs his three clipboards. He looks at the crowd. He looks at the kids. He looks at the kids' parents.

He plays his ace.

He pulls out a fourth piece of paper, a SIGNED OFFER LETTER from the family's bank.

"This family," Drystone says, "is BROKE. Their dock's foundation is GONE. The water is eating it out from under them. They cannot AFFORD the geological work. The bank is calling their loan. Driedmore is the ONLY buyer. We are RESCUING them."

Some of the adults in the crowd nod. Some of the parents close their eyes. This is the part they've been afraid of all morning.

The kids freeze.

This is the real fight. Not the bulldozer. Not the sign. The MONEY. The grown-ups had been told there was no other way and the grown-ups had believed it because the dock IS falling apart. They could SEE it falling apart all week.

Drystone sees the kids' faces. He smiles a thin, mean smile.

"I'm sorry, kids," he says, even though he isn't. "Sometimes the world doesn't work out the way you want. Sometimes the GROWN-UPS have to make the hard call."

Aiden's bottom lip wobbles.

Pegasus, invisible behind the platform, leans her unicorn head against Aiden's shoulder. Aiden steadies.

But then Drystone sees something.

He sees that some of the kids look like the other kids' parents and some of the kids do *not* look like the other kids' parents. He has been studying property records all week. He thinks he knows who is related to whom.

He smiles wider.

He plays his second ace.

## P-551, ★ THE CHOSEN-FAMILY PIVOT ★

*~300 words.*

Drystone walks slowly along the line of kids.

"You know," he says, conversational, "I've been doing my homework on this family. Four families, technically. Some of you are related. Some of you aren't. Princess , " (he gestures at Ella) ",  you and the tall kid are cousins. Real cousins. Your moms are sisters." He gestures at Reid. "But you , " (he gestures at Madison and Kellen) ",  your mom is just a *friend* of theirs. From college. A long time ago. Not blood. And you two, " (he gestures at Aiden and Chayten) ",  same thing. Your mom is a *friend* of a *friend* of the family that actually owns this hotel. Did you know that, sweetheart? Did you know that half of you have no real stake in this place? Your moms don't lose ANYTHING if the actual family sells the property. You're guests. You've ALWAYS been guests."

He turns to the kids' parents.

"Maybe the *blood* family wants to talk to the *guests* about whether they really get to weigh in on a family business decision. Just an idea."

The parents look at each other. The chosen-family moms look first at the blood-family moms. There is a silence Drystone is hoping to grow.

Aiden whispers, "Chayten. Are we GUESTS?"

Chayten goes still.

So does Madison. So does Kellen.

The chosen-family kids are looking at their cousins. The cousins are looking back at them.

Drystone is smiling.

This is the part he is best at, finding the seam and pulling on it.

He waits.

> **Turn to P-552.**

## P-552, A KID TAKES IT APART

*~400 words.*

Carson is the one who moves first.

He doesn't say it loud. He says it the way he says things to Aiden when Aiden is scared.

He says, "You don't know what family is. You also don't know what *belonging* is."

Drystone says, "Excuse me?"

Carson says, "You looked at a property record. You think family is who's on a deed. You think belonging is who pays a mortgage. NONE of us own this place. NONE of our families own it. We're all renters. We rent the same condos every July. We have been renting them since before any of us were born. The cove doesn't belong to us. But WE belong to the cove. And that's something you can't put on a clipboard."

He walks down the line. He doesn't take Drystone's hand. He doesn't gesture dramatically. He just walks along the line of cousins, putting his hand briefly on each kid's shoulder as he passes.

"This is Reid. He's my cousin. We share a grandmother. You can see it on paper. Fine. But this is also Madison." (Hand on Madison's shoulder.) "She's not my cousin on paper. She's been my cousin every July since I was four. She taught me to swim. She made me cookies the summer my dog died. She is MY family. Not because of paper. Because she SHOWED UP."

He keeps walking.

"This is Chayten. Not blood. She braided my hair when I broke my arm. This is Kellen. Not blood. He carried me on his back when I sprained my ankle two summers ago. This is Aiden. He is the LITTLEST of us. He is my COUSIN in EVERY WAY THAT MATTERS. Don't you stand there and tell us who our family is."

He turns back to Drystone.

He is fifteen years old. He is much smaller than Drystone. He is not afraid.

"We chose each other. They chose us. That's stronger than blood. It's stronger because we have to do it again every summer and we ALWAYS DO."

Madison steps up beside him. Then Reid. Then Ella. The blood-family kids and the chosen-family kids are now physically tangled together, arms over shoulders, hands held.

Aiden says, very small, very clear: "We are NOT guests."

Pegasus, behind him, makes a sound that is very close to a unicorn cheering.

Drystone's smile dies on his face.

His attempt to split the family has done the opposite of what he wanted. The kids' *parents* are now also stepping closer to each other. The chosen-family moms and the blood-family moms are standing shoulder to shoulder.

Drystone realizes too late that he has just *cemented* them.

> **Continue to Beat 5.**

### P-560, MAIN (REQUIRES: Stone)

*Kellen the Whole.*

*~350 words.*

Drystone gathers himself. He says, "That's all very SWEET. But the bank offer STANDS. The family, chosen or blood, whatever, still can't AFFORD the geological work. You all line up however you want. The dock will still fall down."

Kellen steps forward.

He has been watching Drystone's posture all morning. He watched where Drystone's hand kept drifting. He watched the way Drystone's left jacket lapel sat slightly heavier than the right.

He has the stone in his palm.

He says, "Mr. Drystone. There's a piece of paper inside your jacket. You touch your lapel every two minutes. I've been counting. Will you take it out?"

Drystone says, "No."

Kellen says, "Okay."

He throws the stone.

The stone skips off the wood of the dock. Bounces off a railing. Skips up the steps of Drystone's platform. Lands on Drystone's inside jacket pocket, through the FABRIC, somehow, the stone went around, and balances on the edge of a hidden fourth clipboard.

The clipboard tips out. It hits the wood. It falls open.

[CONDITIONAL, IF Skim and flock are allies: *Skim and her flock fly in like a small precision air force. They skip stones in formation onto every other piece of paper Drystone has hidden, under his hard hat, in his back pocket, taped to the underside of the bulldozer's steering wheel. Total exposure. Within thirty seconds, every hidden document Drystone owns is in plain sight on the platform.*]

The crowd reads. The contents of the fourth clipboard are Driedmore's internal memo about the bank. About the lowballed appraisal. About a list of three other small hotels Driedmore is doing the same thing to.

The kids' parents are now VERY interested.

Drystone has stopped sweating because his body is no longer producing sweat. His face is the color of paper.

> **Continue to Beat 6.**

### P-561, FALLBACK (NO Stone)

*~300 words.*

Kellen doesn't have the stone. He has the baseball that has been in his pocket since the third of July, the one he was taking shadow swings with when Drystone first arrived, the one he has spent the whole week imagining sending across the cove to the far-off island.

He pulls it out. He bounces it once on the dock, *thock*, and Drystone flinches at the sound. Just a small flinch. But Kellen sees it.

Kellen has been watching Drystone's posture all morning. The shoulder twitch. The lapel pat. The way Drystone's eyes go to the bulldozer when he's lying and to the platform when he's truthful. Kellen is twelve and he has been throwing things into and out of lakes for as long as he can remember; his eyes know what bodies do.

He says, conversationally, "Catch."

## P-602, SILVER ENDING: COME BACK NEXT SUMMER
