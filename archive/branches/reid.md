# Chapter 5 - Reid's Branch (13)

~2,000 words / 15-min read for a 3rd grader.

Lead trait: gentle giant, athletic, knowledgeable, calm. Makes a stick from nearby wood every trip.

## What he earns
**The Unbreakable Staff.** A real wooden staff, carved by Reid using a technique only Old Speak knows. It cannot be broken. Bulldozers bounce off it.

## The task (his prize quest)
Listen to Old Speak's stories - all of them - and tell ONE story back.

## Intro (~300 words)

Reid's raft slides up onto a rocky woodland. His raft is now a long log canoe. He steps off.

He picks up a fallen branch. He starts whittling. He always does this. It's not a thing he thinks about.

A voice from a stump says: "FINALLY."

Reid looks up.

The stump is not a stump. It is a person. A tree-shaped person with bark for skin and leaves growing out of his head. He's enormous - twice Reid's size - but he's sitting down so he doesn't seem scary.

"FINALLY a visitor," the tree-person says. "I've been telling stories to MYSELF for a thousand years. Wait - that's not right. A thousand years AGO I had a visitor and ever since I've been - actually, that reminds me of a JOKE -"

He laughs. Loud. He hasn't told the joke yet.

He laughs again, harder. The trees around him shake.

He gets the joke out: "Why did the river stop running? Because it was TIRED of being CURRENT."

He laughs so hard he tips over.

Reid laughs too. Partly because the joke is bad. Partly because the tree-person's joy is contagious.

The tree-person rights himself. "I'm OLD SPEAK," he says. "Or that's what people called me a thousand years ago. I might have a NEW name now. I haven't checked."

Reid sits down next to him. He keeps whittling.

Old Speak watches.

"You make sticks," he says. "I like sticks."

## Decision 1: How Reid responds to the first story (~300 words)

Old Speak starts telling a story about the time the river ran sideways. It's a bad story. It rambles. He forgets the ending and starts over twice.

- **A. Listen patiently, no interrupting.** (Old Speak is moved. He notices Reid is the first person who didn't try to RUSH him.)
- **B. Ask follow-up questions.** (Old Speak GLOWS. He's never been asked about his own stories before.)
- **C. Laugh at the bad parts.** (Old Speak laughs WITH him. They're now buddies.)

All paths land on Old Speak deciding Reid is the right person to teach the technique to.

## Decision 2: The bad joke contest

Old Speak announces a contest. They will trade bad jokes. Whoever laughs harder loses. Reid laughs first because Old Speak's first joke is so bad it loops into being good.

- **A. Tell a joke he learned from his grandfather.** (Wholesome.)
- **B. Tell a joke he made up on the spot.** (Original.)
- **C. Tell the worst joke he can think of, on purpose.** (Old Speak FALLS over. New best friends.)

## Decision 3: Choosing the wood (Reid's knowledge moment)

Old Speak shows him three pieces of wood, all from different trees in The Old Roar. He tells Reid the choice matters. Reid has to use what he KNOWS about wood and water.

This is the moment Reid's lake knowledge - real, specific, accumulated over summers - shows up. He looks at the three pieces. He picks them up one at a time. He weighs them in his hands. He smells them. He thinks about which would last longest in lake water, which would hold a carving, which would float.

- **A. Maple.** Strong, common, reliable. Reid recognizes maple right away from his stick-making at home. (Solid staff.)
- **B. Black willow.** Bendy, lighter, quick. Reid knows willow from the back dock - the trees that lean over the water. (Lighter staff. Easier to carry.)
- **C. Driftwood from the Roar.** Heavy, scarred, OLD - water-aged for centuries. Reid feels the weight. He smells the lake on it. He knows water-aged wood is the densest, most water-resistant, hardest to break. (Strongest staff. **Has the additional property of stabilizing the dock foundation in the coda** if combined with the right thinking in Decision 4.)

Old Speak nods at whichever one Reid picks. The choice is REAL because the kid is using real knowledge. None of the three are wrong. The Driftwood is the smartest answer.

## Decision 4: The carving technique

Old Speak shows him the technique. It's not what Reid expected. The technique isn't about HOW you cut. It's about what you THINK while you cut.

- **A. Think about home.** (Staff carries warmth. Anyone holding it feels safe at the showdown.)
- **B. Think about the lake.** (Staff carries the lake. **Combined with Driftwood from Decision 3C, the staff can be planted in the cove and it stabilizes the foundation overnight in the coda.**)
- **C. Think about his sister Ella.** (Staff carries protection. Anyone Reid points it at - especially Ella - cannot be hurt at the showdown moment.)

Each path makes the staff slightly different at the showdown. The Driftwood + Lake combination (3C + 4B) is the path that quietly heals the dock foundation in the coda.

## Decision 5: The story trade

When the staff is done, Old Speak says it's Reid's turn. Tell ONE story.

- **A. Tell a true story from the trip.** (Old Speak weeps. **He shows up at the showdown** as a thicket of trees that move into place behind the kids. He counts as the back row of the kids' team.)
- **B. Make up a story.** (Old Speak applauds. He didn't know humans could make things up. Gives Reid a tiny carved staff for a sequel hook.)
- **C. Tell a story he heard from his grandpa.** (Old Speak says "your grandpa sounds wise." Grounds the family theme. Old Speak teaches him a SECOND technique for making baskets out of unbreakable wood - sequel hook.)

## Crescendo close (~300 words)

When Reid finishes his story, Old Speak is quiet for a long moment. Then he picks up the staff Reid carved. He runs his bark-fingers along it. He nods.

"This staff," he says, "cannot be broken. Not by axe, not by fire, not by storm. Not by a man who works for a company called Driedmore Properties."

Reid looks up sharp.

Old Speak smiles. "I hear things. Stumps hear EVERYTHING."

He hands the staff to Reid. Then he reaches into his bark and pulls out a small wooden carving. A miniature staff, the size of a finger.

"That's so you remember me," Old Speak says. "And if you need me - really need me - put the little staff in the dirt. I'll come. I won't be fast. I'm a tree. But I'll come."

Reid pockets the carving. He stands.

"Thank you," he says.

Old Speak waves with leafy fingers. "Tell me a NEW story next time you visit, please. I've memorized this one already."

Reid laughs. He climbs into his log canoe.

As he paddles away, he hears Old Speak still chuckling at his own joke from earlier.

The current sound from the falls is louder now. The lake-up-above is calling.

## How this feeds the showdown
At the data center groundbreaking, Reid's CALM holds the line. The staff is just where his calm lives. He doesn't have to do anything dramatic. He just stands there with the staff and the bulldozer can't move. Reid's quiet weight is what's actually stopping the machine - the unbreakable wood is the visible version of his unmovable presence.

He says, calmly, "It's not going to move." First sentence Reid has spoken in the whole confrontation. Everyone hears it.

If the reader picked Decision 5A, **Old Speak's thicket of trees moves into place behind the kids** during the showdown - magical reinforcements that don't say a word but stand there.

If 3C + 4B (Driftwood + Lake), **the staff stays planted in the cove after the showdown and quietly stabilizes the dock foundation overnight**. Coda mechanic.

## Notes
- Reid is the strongest physically but the most peaceful in tone. His branch celebrates the gentle giant who LISTENS.
- Old Speak's bad jokes are an opportunity for kid-friendly puns throughout the chapter.
- The staff being unbreakable through THOUGHT (not just craft) is the magic.
- Old Speak as a recurring sequel character - he could come visit if Reid ever needs help.
