# Sun Lakes Bingo Card

This is the physical insert that binds into the back of the book. It is also the in-world artifact the children of the book carry into The Old Roar (it is what The Harbor Keeper recognizes — *"prize-earning children. Good. The lake remembers those."*).

The card is a 5×5 grid of real Sun Lakes activities. The center square is a free space. The other 24 squares are tasks the real kids on the real trip can check off across the week.

It is not the same as the in-world quest. It is the *real* version. The book's eight earned things are the magical version of this same idea.

## How to play

Each kid gets their own card. Check off a square when you complete the task. **You don't have to do them in any order.** You don't have to do them all.

But — and this is the rule — **if you get five in a row** (across, down, or diagonal), you earn a prize. The grown-ups decide what the prize is. Past prizes have included: an extra dessert, a later bedtime, getting to pick the next movie, a small Sun Lakes-themed gift.

**Blackout** (all 24 squares plus the free space) earns the kid a special end-of-week prize. Past blackout prizes have included a hand-made walking stick from Reid, a special Pegasus drawing from Keira, and the right to choose the first day's dinner next year.

---

## Adult instructions

Bind this card into the back of the book — perforated, so kids can tear it out. Or print it as a separate folded insert.

For the real Sun Lakes trip, photocopy a stack of these at the beginning of the week and give one to each kid on arrival. The first task they can check off is the FIRST-DAY JUMP-IN free space.

The card is the in-world ancestor of the eight earned things in the book. The book's magic is just the real bingo card with more whimsy.

---

## Notes for the print designer

The card is designed for a single page, landscape orientation. Squares should be large enough for a child to write or stamp in (1.25 inches square minimum). The center square (FIRST-DAY JUMP-IN) should be visually distinct — a small illustration of all 8 kids jumping into the lake at sunset would be perfect, doubling as a callback to the synchronized jump in the framing chapter.

Consider printing on thicker stock so the card survives a week of wet hands and lake water.
