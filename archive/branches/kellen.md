# Chapter 6 - Kellen's Branch (11)

~2,000 words / 15-min read for a 3rd grader.

Lead trait: athletic AND smart - both shine here, no "reveal" of hidden intelligence as a weakness exposed. He's both, the branch celebrates both.

## What he earns
**The True-Skipping Stone.** A flat stone that always skips along whatever surface Kellen calls. Water, dirt, even paper. At the showdown, he uses it to skip across Crumb's permit paperwork - and the stone reveals which line in the contract is wrong.

## The task (his prize quest)
Beat the Skip-Stone Champions at their own game by being BOTH a great thrower AND a great strategist.

## Intro (~300 words)

Kellen's raft slides up to the edge of a long, flat lake. His raft is now a sleek racing canoe. He steps onto the shore and a stone skims past his ear.

It bounces nine times across the water.

He turns. A flock of bird-creatures (long-legged, blue-feathered, smug) are huddled on the bank. They have piles of stones. They are taking turns throwing.

The biggest one notices him.

"Kid," the bird says. "This is a CHAMPIONSHIP game. You can watch from over there." It points with one wing.

Kellen says, "I'd like to play."

All the birds laugh.

The biggest bird says, "This game requires SKILL. And STRATEGY. Most kids only have one or the other. Possibly neither."

Kellen says, "I'll take that bet."

The bird looks intrigued. "It's not a bet. It's a CHALLENGE."

The other birds snicker.

Kellen picks up a stone. He doesn't say anything. He just stands there with the stone.

The biggest bird sighs. "FINE. The game is called Pattern Skip. You CALL a pattern - 'three short, two long, a curve, a stop' - and then you THROW the stone, and it has to skip in that exact pattern. If it does, you score. If it doesn't, you sit out."

Kellen says, "What if I make up a new pattern?"

The bird says, "Nobody MAKES UP patterns. There are SEVEN approved patterns and we use them in CHAMPIONSHIP order."

Kellen says, "What if I made one up that worked?"

The bird stares. "Sit DOWN, kid."

Kellen sits, but he's smiling.

## Decision 1: How to play the first round

The birds let him have a try. He gets one stone. The pattern they call for him is the easiest one ("three skip, three skip, one stop").

- **A. Throw the easy pattern perfectly.** (Birds are surprised but unimpressed. "Beginner's luck.")
- **B. Try a harder pattern than they called for.** (Risky. If he makes it, they're shocked. If he doesn't, they laugh.)
- **C. Throw the easy pattern and add a flourish at the end.** (He performs the basic move with style. The birds are reluctantly impressed.)

## Decision 2: The middle rounds

Kellen plays through the seven approved patterns. He gets every one.

The birds get nervous. The biggest bird pulls him aside.

"Kid," it whispers. "Where did you learn this?"

Kellen shrugs. "I've been throwing balls into the lake my whole life."

- **A. Tell the bird about Hotel Sun Lakes.** (Bird is fascinated. They become weirdly friendly.)
- **B. Show off a little - throw a stone with his eyes closed.** (Bird respects this.)
- **C. Ask the bird how it learned.** (Bird tells him about the Skip-Stone School. Bonding moment.)

## Decision 3: The final challenge

The biggest bird (her name is Skim, Kellen finds out) declares she's the only one left who can challenge him. She'll throw FIRST. Kellen has to MATCH her pattern, then ADD to it.

Skim throws. Eight skips. A curve. A backward bounce. A stop. A SECOND start. A skid.

The other birds gasp.

- **A. Match the pattern exactly and add ONE more skip.** (Safe. Wins by minimum.)
- **B. Match it and add a pattern of his own (something he invents).** (Bold. The birds will lose their minds.)
- **C. Match it and add a pattern that REVERSES Skim's.** (Brilliant. This is the pure-strategy move.)

C is the strongest. The strongest ending sets up his showdown move (where his stone "reads" Crumb's contract).

## Decision 4: When the stone gets to the end

Kellen throws his final stone. He's called the pattern. The stone skips. Skips. Curves. Bounces. The birds are watching with their wings folded.

The stone is about to stop.

It's at the SHORE.

Kellen has to choose how to land it.

- **A. Let it stop naturally.** (Clean win.)
- **B. Have it skid up the bank and stop at Skim's feet.** (Showy. The other birds clap with their wings.)
- **C. Have it skid up the bank, stop, and balance on its EDGE.** (Impossible. But he calls it. And it works. The birds gasp.)

Each version is a slightly different trick at the showdown.

## Decision 5: The prize

Skim concedes. The Skip-Stones don't have a trophy. They have ONE special stone, passed down through champions.

She gives it to Kellen.

"This stone," she says, "always skips true. It does what you tell it. Don't waste it on showing off."

- **A. Promise her he won't.** (Sincere. Just the stone, no ally at showdown.)
- **B. Ask if he can use it to do something IMPORTANT.** (She gives him advice on how to use it - that advice helps him land Crumb's third clipboard at the showdown more cleanly.)
- **C. Ask her to come along if he needs help.** (She thinks about it. **Skim and her flock SHOW UP at the showdown** to skip stones onto every other clipboard Crumb has hidden, exposing all of his fraud at once.)

## Crescendo close (~300 words)

The Skip-Stones throw a small ceremony. Skim gives a speech. The smallest bird trips over its own feet and laughs.

Skim pulls Kellen aside one more time.

"Listen," she says. "Most kids try to be one thing. Strong OR smart. Fast OR clever. You're both. You stopped pretending you weren't smart, and you didn't apologize for being athletic. You just played the WHOLE game."

Kellen pockets the stone.

Skim watches him climb back onto his racing canoe.

"Hey kid," she calls. "What's your name?"

"Kellen."

"Kellen the Kid Who Could." Skim makes a tiny salute with her wing.

Kellen pushes off. He looks back. The Skip-Stones are arguing about who gets to throw first now.

He laughs.

He has a stone in his pocket that always skips true.

## How this feeds the showdown
At the groundbreaking, Crumb tries to argue with Madison's map. He says the map is fake. He says, "Where's your PROOF?"

Kellen's THINKING is what wins this beat. He's the one who knew where Crumb hid the third clipboard - he watched Crumb's posture, watched where his hand kept drifting. The stone is just the messenger Kellen sends to deliver the proof. Anyone could throw a rock; only Kellen knew exactly where to throw THIS one.

He throws. The stone skips off the wood. Bounces off a railing. Skips up the steps of the temporary platform. Lands on Crumb's third clipboard - the SECRET one - and BALANCES on its edge.

The clipboard tips open. The crowd sees what's written there. It matches Madison's map.

If the reader picked Decision 5C, **Skim and her flock fly in** to skip stones onto every clipboard Crumb owns, including the FOURTH one (Crumb's hidden personal one with even more incriminating numbers). Total exposure.

## Notes
- Kellen's branch celebrates BOTH his physicality AND his mind without ever framing one as a "weakness reveal." He plays with his whole self. That's what wins.
- Skim and the Skip-Stones are kid-funny because their trash-talk is mild and their ceremony is short.
- The stone-on-edge trick is the throughline.
