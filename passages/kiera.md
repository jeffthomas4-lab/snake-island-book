## Passage 160 — Arrival at the Painted Cliff

![](images/kiera_region.png)

Kiera's raft slides up to the foot of an enormous cliff. Her raft is now a flat wooden raft with a small easel bolted to it, brushes in a leather holder, and paints in glass jars. The colors are colors Kiera doesn't have names for. She wants names for them immediately.

The cliff in front of her is gigantic. Black basalt, columns rising hundreds of feet up. The columns are perfectly hexagonal, the way good basalt always is. The shadows between them are darker than shadows should be.

She tilts her head.

The cliff has a face.

She tilts the other way.

The cliff is *breathing.*

Just her and the cliff. Her hands are already itching for a brush. The silence here is a different silence, the silence of something asleep, breathing. She has never been alone in a place this big. She finds, to her own surprise, that she likes it. She has been waiting her whole life to look at something this size without someone telling her to hurry up.

She steps off her raft. The cliff doesn't move. But she can hear something, a small sound. Like... snoring? But the snore is *colored.* She can see the color of the snore in the air. Pale blue, with flecks of orange.

She blinks. She still sees the colors.

A small voice from somewhere says, "You're not supposed to be able to see that."

Kiera looks around. There's nobody.

The voice says, "I'm UP HERE. Look at the third column from the left."

She looks. The third basalt column has eyes. The eyes are closed.

The cliff is asleep.

He has been asleep, she figures out, for a very long time.

He says (without opening his eyes), "I haven't had a visitor in five hundred years. Are you a visitor or are you a dream? I get them confused."

Kiera says, "I'm a visitor."

The cliff says, "*Wonderful.* Could you do me a favor? I would very much like to wake up."

Kiera says, "How?"

The cliff says, "I don't know. I've been trying for centuries. Maybe a fresh perspective."

He yawns. The yawn is purple.

## Passage 161 — The Both-at-once Moment

Before Kiera even picks up a brush, the cliff opens one eye, just a crack.

He looks at her. Closes it again.

"Huh," he says.

Kiera says, "What?"

"You have an artist's eye. I can see it. But you ALSO have a climber's body. Tall for your age. Strong shoulders. I can see THAT too. Most visitors are one or the other. You're both. All the way down."

Kiera shrugs. "I just like to do everything."

"That is RARE," the cliff says. He sounds delighted. "Most of the world makes you pick. Most of the world tells the artist to stop climbing and the climber to stop painting. *You haven't picked.* I can tell. Don't ever pick."

Kiera doesn't know what to say. She feels her face get warm in a way that's pleasant.

She is ten years old. People have been telling her she's *good for an artist* or *good for her age* her whole life. Nobody has ever told her she gets to be everything she is all at once.

"Okay," she says.

"Good," the cliff says. "Now. Please paint me awake. I have been BORED for two hundred of the last five hundred years and I would like to see the sky again."

"And one more thing, child," Cliffy says, his enormous bark face turned toward her. "You are Kiera Both-at-Once. Artist AND climber. The cliff will remember that you refused to pick. So should you."

He yawns purple.

Kiera opens her paint jars.

The paints inside *move* when she opens them. They lean toward her. They are excited to be used.

She picks up a brush.

## Passage 163 — Just Start Painting
Kiera shrugs and picks up the orange. The orange leaps out of the jar. She catches it with the brush. It is *eager.*

She paints. Fast. The way she paints when nobody is watching, wide strokes, no plan, the colors deciding their own next move. The orange leads. The blue follows. A green she doesn't recognize joins in halfway through.

The cliff is purring now. Each stroke wakes him a little more. His snore has stopped. His breathing has slowed.

She gets to his eyes, they are thirty feet up.

She climbs.

This is the part of the chapter that should celebrate Kiera's other half. She's been jumping off the small cliff with the older boys for two summers. She studies the seams in the basalt. She finds the cracks where her fingers will fit. She goes up.

The mouse with spectacles (a tiny creature who has materialized on a ledge and is keeping notes in a tiny book) watches her with both paws over its mouth.

She reaches Cliffy's eye. She paints a small ring of color around it. Then a small ring around the other. She perches on his cheekbone.

She says, "Okay. Open."

Cliffy opens his eyes.

The cliff has eyes.

He looks at her. She looks at him. He smiles. The whole cliff face smiles.

She climbs down. Slowly. Carefully. She gets to the bottom.

Cliffy reaches into his basalt chest and pulls out a small brush. The brush is wood, with a tip made of light.

"This," he says, "is a Truth Brush. You don't paint with it. You look through it. Anything hidden becomes visible. Lies turn yellow. Truth glows blue."

Kiera takes it. It fits in her pocket.

The mouse with spectacles writes the climb into the cliff's history book. He is, she will learn later, the official biographer of Cliffy. He has been waiting for something to record for two hundred years.

*If the reader has played Reid's branch, the mouse pauses and adds a note: "tree-being walking, west side." He shows it to her. "He doesn't usually walk," the mouse says. "Big day."*

She climbs onto her raft. Cliffy waves. The mouse waves. The paints in her jars settle.

## Passage 164 — Ask What He Wants
![](images/canyon_waking.png)

Kiera says, "What do you want to be painted AS?"

Cliffy is quiet for a very long time.

He says, "Nobody has ever asked me that."

She waits.

He says, slowly, "I don't think I know what I look like. I've been asleep so long. What does the world look like now? What's NEW? What would I want to wear?"

Kiera sits down on a flat rock at his foot. She leans against him. He is cool and steady. She thinks.

"What about... kindness?" she says. "What does kindness look like? I'll paint that."

Cliffy goes very still.

"Kindness," he says. "I haven't thought about that in five hundred years. I... think kindness looks like... one hand reaching toward another hand. Yes? Is that right?"

Kiera nods. She thinks so. She thinks that's exactly right.

She paints. Up his basalt forearm, a long curving stroke of warm gold, a hand. Up his other forearm, the other hand, reaching. She paints them so the fingertips don't quite touch. They are reaching, always reaching, never finishing the reach. The reach is the thing.

When she steps back, Cliffy is crying.

His tears are pale blue paint. They drip down his basalt face and pool at his feet. He is overwhelmed.

"That is the most beautiful thing," he whispers, "that has ever been on me."

She climbs to his eyes. She is not afraid. Her hands know which seams in the basalt will hold and her feet follow. She paints a small ring around each. She perches on his cheekbone.

"Open."

He opens his eyes.

He looks down at his own arms. He sees the hands. He sees them reaching toward each other.

He cries again. More blue paint.

He pulls a Truth Brush out of his basalt chest and gives it to her. This one is older, more worn, the wood polished by something Kiera can't quite see.

"This is mine. It was my mother's. She was a cliff too. You should have it. You earned it."

Then he says, very quietly: "I cannot leave this place. I am stone in this ground. But, if you call my name from somewhere with stone, my *attention* will travel. I will be there in spirit. The basalt around your home will notice. The man with the clipboards, he won't see me. But he will feel watched. That is all I can offer."

Kiera says, "That's a lot."

Cliffy says, "It is what I have."

*Three operatic notes carry across the canyon. Cliffy's whole face turns toward the sound. "That woman has been singing here for as long as I've been awake," he says. "Wait, I just woke up. As long as I was asleep. She sang to me. I always thought I was dreaming her."*

She climbs down. He bows slightly. The mouse with spectacles is writing furiously.

## Passage 165 — Climb First
Kiera looks up the cliff face. Up. Up. Up.

She says, "I'm going to climb up first. I want to see what you've been dreaming."

Cliffy makes a sound that is somewhere between a yawn and a laugh.

"Climb me? CLIMB me? No one climbs me. I am a CLIFF. I am hundreds of feet tall."

Kiera is already climbing.

She finds the seams. The basalt is forgiving. The columns are perfectly hexagonal and her fingers know exactly where to wedge. She climbs steadily, not fast, not slow, the way she climbs the rocks at home, the way she climbs the small cliff the older boys jump off.

She is fifty feet up before Cliffy realizes she is *actually doing this.*

He goes very still so as not to dislodge her.

"Child," he says, very carefully. "What are you doing."

"Climbing."

"You are TEN."

"Tall for my age."

She keeps going. The mouse with spectacles is at the base of the cliff, biting his tiny knuckles. The paints in their jars are watching her too.

A hundred feet. Two hundred. Three hundred. She gets to a ledge near his eyes and stops. She sits on it. She catches her breath.

The view from up here is not the view from below.

She can see what Cliffy has been dreaming. The canyon, the whole canyon, has color *in the rock.* Veins of blue. Veins of orange. Streaks of green where the basalt should be black. The color is *there,* under the surface, invisible from the ground. The cliffs have been dreaming color this whole time and the color is still in them, waiting.

"Oh," Kiera says. "Oh."

Cliffy says, quietly, "You see them."

"You're SO COLORFUL."

"I forgot. I'd forgotten. For so long I was just black rock to everyone. I forgot the color was still in me. Even I had stopped seeing it."

Kiera opens a jar of paint she brought up clipped to her belt. She paints around his eyes. She uses the colors she sees in the rock, *his* colors, the ones he forgot. Blue. Orange. Green.

"Open," she says.

He opens his eyes.

And the canyon WAKES.

Color floods up the cliff face. Every basalt column from the water to the sky lights up, blue veins, orange streaks, green ribbons. The whole canyon, both walls, miles of basalt, turns colored at once. It is *enormous.* It is the size of the whole world.

Kiera can hear, faintly, every basalt cliff in The Old Roar starting to hum. The wakeup is contagious. Cliffs that have been asleep for thousands of years are stretching.

And then she sees something nobody else will ever see.

For one second, just one, there is a *face* in the canyon wall opposite. Made of veins. Blue and orange. A woman's face, the basalt arranged like cheekbones, the orange streaks shaped like an open mouth. The face is singing. Kiera can't hear it from this far, but she can see the shape of the singing, the mouth open, the throat working, the face full of the joy of a person who didn't know anyone was listening.

The face fades into ordinary colored rock.

Kiera does not tell anyone. She knows already that this was meant for her. She has the artist's eye. The lake had been waiting for someone with an artist's eye to *notice.* Someday, maybe at home, maybe at this lake, maybe many summers from now, she will hear that face singing for real. She will know who it is. She will not say.

The mouse with spectacles drops his pen.

Cliffy, very slowly, raises a hand to his face and feels his own colored cheekbones.

He says, "You woke up the WHOLE CANYON."

Kiera says, "You were already this colorful. I just looked."

He carries her down, gently, his hand the size of a small house. He sets her on her feet. He pulls a Truth Brush from his chest and hands it to her. The light at the tip is brighter than the other versions. It looks like a small contained sunrise.

"I am COMING with you," he says. "I am going to walk to the lake-up-above. I am a CLIFF. This will take many hours. I will be slow. I do not care. I am coming. The basalt around your cove is my family. I want to stand with my family."

*A small bright shape gallops along a ridge of the freshly-colored canyon, glimpsed for two seconds, a unicorn with a small boy on her back. Cliffy laughs (the laugh is enormous and colored). "There's the boy who never stopped believing," he says. "He's part of this. Good."*

Kiera climbs onto her raft. Behind her, miles of basalt are humming in color. The paints in her jars are leaping with joy.


> *This is Kiera's CROWN ending.*

## Passage 166 — Exit

The raft is waiting. The paints settle. The Truth Brush sits in her pocket warm against her thigh.


She has the Brush. She has, somewhere behind her ribs, the new and powerful feeling that the world has been more colorful all along than she'd been allowed to see.

She paddles toward the sound of the C-130, faint and far, somewhere up in the world above.
