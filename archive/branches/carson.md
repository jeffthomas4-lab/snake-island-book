# Chapter 4 - Carson's Branch (13)

~2,000 words / 15-min read for a 3rd grader.

Lead trait: super kind and athletic, competitive AND fun-loving, protective older cousin to Aiden. Loves the entire week.

## What he earns
**The Tusk-Charm.** A piece of mammoth tusk, smooth and warm, that gives him a rallying voice. When he speaks at the showdown, the OTHER kids fall in behind him without being asked. Plus: Big Coulee herself promises to come if he calls.

## The task (his prize quest)
Beat Big Coulee in the Coulee Games' Long Race. (Or tie. Or share. Real branching.)

## Intro (~300 words)

Carson's raft slides to a stop on a wide grassy plain. His raft has become a sturdy wooden boat that rolls on tiny wheels. He hops out.

He hears thumping. Big thumping.

A wooly mammoth jogs past him, chest out, trunk held high, talking to herself.

"AND BIG COULEE rounds the bend," she announces, "looking STRONG. Looking FAST. Looking like the CHAMPION she was BORN to be."

She jogs another lap. She's wearing a flower crown. The crown is too small for her head and tilts to one side.

She sees Carson. She skids to a stop.

"CHILD," she says. "Are you here for the GAMES?"

Carson says he doesn't know what the games are.

She gasps. She tells him: every year the Coulee Games happen, and the Long Race is the main event, and SHE has been training for SIX MOONS, and her two babies (BABY mammoths! she introduces them - they are named Tuft and Tumble) keep WANDERING OFF, and her trainer (a small bird who sits on her ear) says she's READY but she's NERVOUS, and she's so glad Carson is here because the race is in 30 minutes and SOMEONE needs to be the OTHER racer or there's no game.

She looks at him with big mammoth eyes.

Carson grins.

"I'll race you," he says.

## Decision 1: How to make the race more fun

Big Coulee is bigger and stronger. A straight race, she wins. A clever race, who knows.

- **A. Race with NO rules.** (Pure speed. Big Coulee wins by a lot. They have a great time.)
- **B. Race AND gather Tuft and Tumble along the way - whoever has more babies at the end wins.** (Strategy + protection. Carson can win this one.)
- **C. Race backward - last one to the finish wins.** (Goofy. Big Coulee LOVES this idea.)

The "competitive AND fun" trait says he proposes a smart competitive game. B is the strongest route. C is the funniest. A is the warmest.

## Decision 2: The route

There are three paths through the Coulee.

- **A. The Fast Path.** Open grassland. Wind in his face.
- **B. The Scenic Path.** Through wildflowers. Slower but he can scoop up Tuft and Tumble who are hiding among the blooms.
- **C. The Thicket Path.** A maze of old branches. Dangerous-looking but full of mammoth babies who got stuck.

If he picked B in Decision 1, he'll want B or C in Decision 2 to find the babies. If he picked A in Decision 1, the path doesn't matter as much.

## Decision 3: Big Coulee falls

Halfway through, Big Coulee trips on her flower crown (which has slid completely over one eye). She tumbles. She's okay but she's down.

- **A. Keep racing.** (She'd want him to. He wins. She's proud.)
- **B. Stop and help her up.** (He loses speed. She's grateful. Big Coulee says the trip-and-fall was the BEST PART because Carson came back.)
- **C. Fix her flower crown.** (Tiny gesture. She loves this. She tells the story for the rest of the day.)

The protective-cousin trait pulls toward B or C. The competitive trait toward A. The book lets ALL paths work. Carson can choose.

## Decision 4: The final stretch

He's near the finish line. Tuft and Tumble are riding on his shoulders (they're as big as dogs). Big Coulee is right behind.

- **A. Sprint.** (He wins by a step. Big Coulee yells "WORTHY OPPONENT" and laughs.)
- **B. Slow down so they cross together.** (They tie. Big Coulee is overcome with emotion. The Coulee Games' rule book has no provision for ties. The judge bird short-circuits in confusion.)
- **C. Step aside and let her win.** (She refuses. She picks him up in her trunk and CARRIES him over the line beside her.)

## Decision 5: The prize

At the winner's circle, the bird trainer says they have one prize. A tusk-charm carved from Big Coulee's grandmother's tusk. It can only be given to a TRUE CHAMPION.

The bird looks at Big Coulee. Looks at Carson. Looks confused.

Big Coulee solves it.

- **A. She gives the tusk-charm to Carson.** ("He's the kid. He needs it more." Carson gets the charm, no showdown ally.)
- **B. They share - she breaks the tusk-charm in half.** (Wholesome. **Big Coulee SHOWS UP at the showdown**, thundering down the dock road with her flower crown still on. Crumb faints.)
- **C. Carson refuses and asks her to keep it.** (Big Coulee tears up. She gives him her promise to come if called. **Big Coulee shows up at the showdown WITH Tuft and Tumble**. Three mammoths on the dock.)

## Crescendo close (~300 words)

After the race, the Coulee Games turn into a party. Big Coulee teaches Carson to dance the Mammoth Stomp. Tuft and Tumble climb on his head. The bird trainer makes a speech about how this was the BEST Coulee Games in living memory and writes Carson's name in the official records.

Big Coulee walks Carson back to his raft. The boat is back on the bank, wheels and all.

She kneels (slowly) so her face is level with his.

"Listen, kid," she says. "You raced like a champion. You also stopped for me when I fell. You picked up my babies. You shared the win." She tucks the tusk-charm into his hand. "When you go back to the lake-up-above, you remember this: you can be a CHAMPION and a FRIEND at the same time. Most grown-ups forget that. Don't you forget it."

Carson nods. Big Coulee touches her flower crown to his forehead.

"Call me if you need me," she says. "I have very loud lungs."

Carson climbs onto the boat-raft.

He looks back. Big Coulee is doing one last training loop, Tuft and Tumble bouncing along beside her. She's narrating again: "AND BIG COULEE rounds the bend, having WON, having SHARED, having LIVED."

Carson laughs. He paddles away.

## How this feeds the showdown
Carson's PRESENCE rallies the kids. After Madison cracks the story and Chayten holds the crowd, Carson steps in front of the bulldozer and his cousins and chosen-cousins all line up beside him without him saying a word. The tusk-charm glows but the team is forming because of HIM, not the charm. The charm is just a visible flag of what was already true.

If the reader picked Decision 5B, **Big Coulee thunders down the dock road** to physically intimidate Crumb.

If 5C, **Big Coulee plus Tuft and Tumble** show up - three mammoths on the dock is a sight nobody at the groundbreaking will forget.

## Notes
- "Champion AND friend at the same time" is the through-line. Carson doesn't have to choose between competing and caring. He's BOTH.
- Big Coulee narrating her own life is a recurring kid-funny gag.
- Tuft and Tumble are tiny side characters who could come back in a sequel.
