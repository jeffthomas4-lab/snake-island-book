## Passage 180 — Arrival in the Unicorn Meadow

![](images/aiden_region.png)

Aiden's raft is the unicorn raft.

He climbed on in the sunroom. Now he is flying.

The big plastic unicorn is not plastic anymore. She is real. Her mane is a rainbow. Her hooves do not quite touch the ground.

She turns her big head. She looks at him over her shoulder.

"LOOK AT THIS!" she says. She points her horn at a cloud. The cloud looks like a fish.

Aiden says, "Yeah!"

"AND LOOK AT THIS!" she says. Now she points at a rainbow. The rainbow is growing sideways.

Aiden says, "Wow!"

She lands soft in a meadow. The grass is so green. Aiden slides off her back.

Just him. He should be scared. He is not. Chayten is somewhere else. Carson is somewhere else. Even Madison is somewhere else. He is alone on a green meadow with a real unicorn. He thinks one thing. *Someone trusted me with this.* No one has ever trusted him with a thing this big. He stands up a little taller.

Pegasus bows her head. They are eye to eye.

"I have waited a long time," she says. "Two hundred years. Do you know why? No one was the right size. The other riders were too big. Too tall. Too old. Some of them did not believe in unicorns enough. Then today, I felt a small boy climb on me in a sunroom. And I thought, *oh. There he is.*"

She tells him her name. She has never said it out loud before.

"Pegasus."

Aiden says, "But Pegasus is a HORSE not a UNICORN."

Pegasus says, "I picked it MYSELF."

Aiden laughs. Pegasus is so happy.

## Passage 181 — The Exactly-right Moment

Then Pegasus gets serious. As serious as a unicorn with a color-changing mane can get.

"Aiden," she says. "I need to tell you a thing. Sit down."

Aiden sits in the grass.

Pegasus folds her legs. She lies down across from him. Her horn glows a little.

"You are Aiden the Exactly-Right," she says. "EXACTLY RIGHT."

Aiden says, "For what?"

"For me. For this. For today. You are not too small. You are not too young. You are the right boy. The world will tell you you are too little. The world will be wrong. *I picked you.* Out of everyone. Because you fit."

Aiden's chin lifts a little.

Then Pegasus boops his nose with her horn.

The horn is warm. A little tickly.

Aiden laughs.

Pegasus says, "Now. There is a problem. A baby otter fell into a magic hole. Only a unicorn can save her. But I am too young to save her by myself. I need a rider. Will you ride with me?"

Aiden does not stop to think.

"Yes."

Pegasus stands up. She flicks her rainbow mane. She bows. He climbs on.

## Passage 183 — the Meadow of Bells
Pegasus flies them to the Meadow of Bells. The name is just right. Every flower is a tiny bell. When the wind blows, the meadow rings.

Aiden gasps. It is so pretty.

The magic hole is under a big bell-flower. They find it fast. The flower lifts up out of the way. The hole goes down into the ground.

They hear a baby otter cry.

"Hold on tight," Pegasus says.

Aiden grips her mane. Pegasus folds her wings. They dive.

Down they go. The hole opens into a shiny cave. Crystals glow on the walls. In the middle sits Ripple the otter. Three weeks old. Soft. Scared. Small enough to fit in Aiden's two hands.

She has cried for a long time.

Aiden slides off Pegasus. He walks over. He kneels down.

"Hi," he says. "I am Aiden. We came to get you."

Ripple sniffs his hand. Then she climbs into his pocket and stays there.

Pegasus lifts her horn. It glows. Four doors are at the back of the cave. One door glows back. That is the way out.

They fly up. They come out in the meadow. Ripple's mother is waiting. She cries. Then she squeaks with joy. She touches her nose to Aiden's nose.

"You saved her," she says. "Thank you."

*If you have read Reid's chapter, the bells of the meadow ring once together. Like a chord. Pegasus tilts her head. "Trees walking," she says. "A big tree-man is moving today. The bells know him."*

Aiden hands Ripple to her mother. Ripple chirps a small goodbye in his hand.

Pegasus says, "Time to go home, friend."

Aiden climbs on. He says, "Will you come with me? To the lake-up-above?"

Pegasus thinks. (She thinks fast.) "Yes."

He says, "Will it be scary?"

She says, "Yes. But you will be there. And Madison. And Chayten. And the others."

Aiden nods. He grips her mane.

They fly back toward the C-130. The sound is soft and far. Somewhere up in the world above.

"LOOK AT THIS!" Pegasus says. She points at a cloud that looks like a sandwich.

Aiden laughs all the way home.

## Passage 184 — the Cliffside
Pegasus flies them to the cliff. The cliff is steep. The rock is dark. The hole is at the bottom. They land on a small ledge. They walk to the door.

Inside, Ripple is crying. But another sound is there too. A small one. Under the crying.

Pegasus folds her wings small. Aiden walks beside her. Her horn glows just enough to see.

The cave opens up. Four doors at the back. Ripple sits in the middle. In front of one door sits a very small, very grumpy thing.

He is the size of a kitten. He has spikes that are not pointy. He looks more sad than scary.

"Do not go through my door. It is MY door. I LIVE here."

Aiden kneels. "Hi. What is your name?"

"Hush."

"Hush. I am Aiden. We need to go through your door. Ripple's mom is waiting."

Hush starts to cry. His tears are tiny and glowing.

"Everyone wants to go through my door. No one ever just SITS with me. I have been here for FIVE HUNDRED YEARS. No one has ever sat with me."

Aiden sits down. He looks at Pegasus. Pegasus nods.

"I will sit with you. Will you sit with me?"

Hush stops crying mid-sob. "...Yes? Yes. *Yes.*"

Aiden opens his arms. Hush climbs into his lap. He weighs almost nothing. He smells like cave moss. He puts his small grumpy face on Aiden's chest. He shakes.

They sit. Ripple watches from the middle of the cave. Quiet.

Then Pegasus says a thing Aiden did not see coming.

"Aiden. Listen. The Hush has been alone for five hundred years. *Five hundred.* He cannot come back with us. He is tied to this cave. The door only opens because *he* opens it. If he leaves, the door closes for good. The cave seals up. No lost creature will ever be found again."

Aiden looks down at Hush. Hush is so quiet on his chest.

"But." Pegasus says it soft. "He could come if *I* stay."

Aiden looks up.

"A unicorn for a doorkeeper. Magic for magic. The cave would stay open. The Hush would get to come with you. But I would have to stay."

Aiden's eyes fill up. He does not want to pick. He wanted Pegasus to come home so much. He has not let himself think about anything else for an hour.

Pegasus lowers her head. "It is your choice, friend. I will be glad with either one."

The cave is very quiet.

Aiden looks at Hush. He looks at Pegasus. He looks at Ripple in the middle.

He thinks for a long minute. Longer than seven-year-olds usually think about a thing.

Then he says, quiet: "Pegasus, you said I was exactly right. I think being exactly right means I do not get to keep you just for me today."

Pegasus closes her eyes.

Aiden says, "But, can I think one more second?"

Pegasus says, "Take all the seconds."

Aiden thinks. He looks at Hush. Then he says: "Hush. Could you come for one day? Just one? To help us at the dock-up-above? And then come back? Would that work?"

Hush sits up. He looks at Pegasus. Pegasus looks surprised. Then *happy.*

"You, you could leave for ONE DAY?" Pegasus asks Hush. "Not for good?"

Hush thinks. "One day is a lot less than five hundred years. I can do one day."

Pegasus says, "Then the door stays open for that day. Magic lets a visit. It does not let a move."

Aiden laughs. He fixed a problem that was not supposed to be fixed.

Hush climbs to Aiden's shoulder. He has gone from sad to *brave* in about three minutes. He sits taller on Aiden's shoulder than he has sat in five hundred years. He even tries a small spike-stretch. He has never done that before. He wants to look a little tougher.

"Look," he whispers to Aiden. He sounds wowed at himself. "I am, I am DOING things. I never DO things. You made me do things."

Aiden says, like it is plain fact, "You did. You were always going to. You just needed someone to ask."

Hush thinks about that for a long moment. It is the kind of moment a small creature has when something inside him moves over for good.

Pegasus bumps Aiden's head with her horn.

"You found the third way," she says. "Most grown-ups can't. Most kids can. You just did. And look. You made him brave by treating him like he was brave already."

Aiden grins. He did not think he had made anyone brave. But he had.

*Above the cliff, a small flat stone skips across the sky in a clean pattern. Hush tilts his small grumpy head. "Some things fly here that should not. I never ask why."*

They come up in the meadow. Ripple's mother cries. Hush rides Aiden's shoulder. Pegasus flies them home.

## Passage 185 — the River's Bend
Pegasus flies them to the River's Bend. The river is wide and warm. The otters have been waiting.

A tea party on a flat rock in the middle of the river. A tiny driftwood table. A tea pot the size of a thimble. Ripple's mother is there. Calm. The otters have kept her company.

"We knew you would come. Have tea. Then we rescue her."

Aiden looks at Pegasus. Pegasus shrugs. "We have a few minutes."

The head otter is small. Grey on her face. She wears a kelp necklace. Her name is Aunt Stream. She does not look like she is here for small talk.

"Child. Before we start. There is something you need to know about the river. The rescue is not free."

Aiden sets his thimble of tea down.

"To open the hole, someone has to give the river something that matters. Not gold. Not magic. *Something that matters to you.* That is the river's price."

Aiden looks at Pegasus. Pegasus is very still.

"What do I have that matters?"

Aunt Stream looks at him kind. "You have the unicorn."

Aiden's chin starts to wobble.

"No. Not her staying. Not her belonging to you. The river will not take a creature. The river takes a *promise.* Pegasus is a unicorn. Unicorns belong to the whole world. Not to one boy. If you bring her home, you have to promise that she gets to come back. Not just to visit. She gets to come *back*. When the work at your lake is done. She does not stay with you for good."

Aiden looks at Pegasus. Pegasus is looking back at him.

He says, in a very small voice, "When does she come back?"

"When she is ready. Maybe a year. Maybe ten. Maybe when you are a grown-up. The river does not say. The river just asks for the promise."

Aiden thinks about this for a long time. The otters are quiet. Ripple's mother is quiet. The river is louder than it was.

He says, "I will promise."

Pegasus says, "Aiden."

"It is right. You belong to everyone. You are a UNICORN. You waited two hundred years for me. I can wait some years for someone else to need you next. You should get to find them."

Pegasus puts her horn on his head. Her eyes are wet.

Aunt Stream says, "Good. The river takes it."

The river hums. The rescue hole opens itself under them.

Then Aunt Stream gets a little quieter. "Child. While we have you. Do you ever hear singing? At night? At your lake-up-above?"

Aiden thinks. "Yes. Two summers ago. A LADY was singing. We never knew who. Mom said it was nobody."

Aunt Stream nods, slow. "It was *somebody.* Your mother is more shy than she lets on. Pass it on. But gently. The singer should know we heard her. When the time is right. Not yet."

Aiden tucks that away in the part of his brain that holds important things.

They dive. They find Ripple. They carry her up.

Pegasus and Aiden fly home.

On the way back, Pegasus says: "Aiden. You did the bravest thing of all eight of you today. You let me go before I went."

Aiden, quiet: "I did not let you go. I let you visit other people. That is different."

Pegasus laughs the loudest unicorn laugh in two hundred years.

*Above the river, the basalt cliffs are streaked with color. Blue. Orange. Green. The colors were not there an hour ago. "The painter girl woke them," Aunt Stream says. "Big day."*

## Passage 186 — Exit

The sky is purple and gold. The C-130 is rumbling up ahead. It is calling them home.

He has Pegasus. He may have a friend on his shoulder. He may have a song in his ears. He may have just a unicorn at his back.

He always has Pegasus.

Pegasus says, "LOOK AT THIS!"

She is pointing at the sun coming through the canyon.

Aiden says, "Yeah."
