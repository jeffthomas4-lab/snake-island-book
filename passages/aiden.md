## P-180 — ARRIVAL IN THE UNICORN MEADOW

*~350 words. Slight variant per wrapper.*

Aiden's raft is the unicorn raft.

He climbed onto it in the sunroom and now he's flying.

The big inflatable unicorn used to be plastic. Now she's real. Her mane is rainbow. Her hooves don't quite touch the ground.

She turns her big head and looks at Aiden over her shoulder.

"LOOK AT THIS!" she says. (She points with her horn at a cloud shaped like a fish.)

Aiden says, "Yeah!"

She says, "AND LOOK AT THIS!" (Now she's pointing at a rainbow that's growing sideways.)

Aiden says, "Wow!"

She lands gently in a meadow. The grass is greener than grass should be. Aiden slides off her back.

[VARIANT: STAY-TOGETHER. *The other seven kids are landing in the meadow too, their rafts coming down softly around him. Carson catches Aiden's hand as he slides off, automatic. Chayten is already looking around to make sure he's okay. He waves at both of them. He's fine. He is FANTASTIC. He is also, he realizes, the only one of them on an actual unicorn.*]

[VARIANT: SPLIT. *Three other kids are with him. Chayten is one of them — she came in his group on purpose, and she is standing close enough that he can grab her hand without reaching. Carson is the second; he is the cousin who always lifts Aiden onto things, and Aiden can already tell he wants to be the one to lift him onto Pegasus the first time. Reid is the third, calm as he always is, watching the meadow.*]

[VARIANT: SOLO. *Just him. He should be scared. He isn't. Chayten is somewhere else and Carson is somewhere else and even Madison is somewhere else, and he is alone on a green meadow with a unicorn that just turned real, and the only thing he can think is: somebody trusted me with this. He has never been trusted with something this big. He stands up a little straighter.*]

Pegasus bows her head so they're eye to eye.

"I have been waiting for a rider for two hundred years," she says. "Did you know that? Do you want to know why? Because no one else FIT. The riders were too BIG. Or too TALL. Or too OLD. Or they didn't believe in unicorns enough. And then today I felt a small boy climbing onto me in a sunroom and I thought — *OH. There he is.*"

She tells him her name. She has never said it out loud before.

"Pegasus."

Aiden says, "But Pegasus is a HORSE not a UNICORN."

Pegasus says, "I picked it MYSELF."

Aiden laughs. Pegasus is delighted.

## P-181 — THE EXACTLY-RIGHT MOMENT

*~250 words. Always read.*

Then Pegasus gets serious. As serious as a unicorn whose mane is currently changing color can be.

"Aiden," she says. "I need to tell you a thing. Sit down."

Aiden sits in the grass.

Pegasus folds her legs under her and lies down across from him. Her horn glows faintly.

"You are EXACTLY RIGHT," she says.

Aiden says, "For what?"

"For ME. For this. For everything happening today. You are not too small. You are not too young. You are the *exact right person* for what we are about to do. The world will keep telling you you're too little to matter. The world will be wrong about that for the rest of your life. I want you to know, right now, before anything happens, that the world is wrong. *I picked you.* Out of everyone. Because you fit."

Aiden's chin wobbles a little.

Then Pegasus boops his nose with her horn.

The horn is warm and slightly tickly.

Aiden laughs.

Pegasus says, "Now. There's a problem in The Old Roar. A baby otter fell into a magic hole. Only a unicorn can save her. But I am too YOUNG to save her by myself. I need a rider. Will you ride with me?"

Aiden doesn't even hesitate.

"Yes."

Pegasus stands up. She flicks her rainbow mane. She bows for him to climb on.

Aiden climbs on.

## P-183 — PATH A: THE MEADOW OF BELLS (BASE)

*~550 words. Yields Pegasus (base).*

Pegasus flies them to the Meadow of Bells. It is exactly what it sounds like: a wide meadow where every flower is a tiny bell. When the wind blows, the meadow rings.

Aiden gasps because it is beautiful.

The magic hole is hidden under a particularly large bell-flower. They find it quickly. The flower lifts itself out of the way for Pegasus. The hole goes down into the earth.

They can hear a baby otter crying.

"Hold on tight," Pegasus says.

Aiden grips her mane. Pegasus folds her wings and dives.

Down they go. The hole opens out into a glittering cave. Crystals glow on the walls. Sitting in the middle of the cave is Ripple the otter — three weeks old, fuzzy, scared, small enough to fit in Aiden's two hands.

She has been crying for what looks like hours.

Aiden slides off Pegasus. He walks over. He kneels down.

"Hi," he says. "I'm Aiden. We came to get you."

Ripple sniffs his hand. Then she climbs into his pocket and stays there.

Pegasus lifts her horn to glow. Of the four doors at the back of the cave, one glows back. That's the way out.

They fly up the passage. They surface in the meadow. Ripple's mother is waiting — frantic, then sobbing, then squeaking with relief. She touches her nose to Aiden's nose.

"You saved her," she says. "Thank you."

[CROSSOVER HOOK: *If the reader has played Reid's branch, the bells of the meadow ring once in a deep cluster, like a chord. Pegasus tilts her head. "Trees walking," she says. "There's a tree-being moving today. Big tree. The bells know him."*]

Aiden hands Ripple to her mother. Ripple chirps a small goodbye into his hand.

Pegasus says, "Time to go home, friend."

Aiden climbs on. He says, "Will you come with me? To the lake-up-above?"

Pegasus thinks. (She thinks fast.) "Yes."

He says, "Will it be scary?"

She says, "Yes. But you'll be there. And Madison. And Chayten. And the others."

Aiden nods. He grips her mane.

They fly back toward the sound of the C-130, faint and far, somewhere up in the world above.

"LOOK AT THIS!" Pegasus says, pointing at a cloud that looks like a sandwich.

Aiden laughs all the way home.

**→ Exit to P-186.**

## P-184 — PATH B: THE CLIFFSIDE (ENHANCED — A REAL CHOICE)

*~700 words. Yields Pegasus + Hush + a cost. This is where the path actually tests Aiden.*

Pegasus flies them to the cliffside. The cliff is steep, basalt, and dark. The hole is at its base. They have to land on a small ledge and walk to the entrance.

Inside, Ripple is crying. But there's another sound too. Something smaller. Something underneath the crying.

Pegasus folds her wings small. Aiden walks at her side. Her horn glows just enough to see by.

The cave opens out. Four doors at the back. Ripple is in the middle. And in front of one of those doors sits a very small, very grumpy creature.

He is the size of a kitten. Spikes that aren't actually pointy. Sad more than scary.

"Don't go through my door. It's MY door. I LIVE here."

Aiden kneels. "Hi. What's your name?"

"Hush."

"Hush. I'm Aiden. We need to go through your door. Ripple's mom is waiting."

Hush starts to cry. Glowing tiny tears.

"Everyone always WANTS to go through my door. Nobody ever just SITS with me. I have been here for FIVE HUNDRED YEARS and nobody has ever just SAT WITH ME."

Aiden sits down. He looks at Pegasus. Pegasus nods.

"I'll sit with you. Would you like to sit with me?"

Hush stops crying mid-sob. "...Yes? Yes. *Yes.*"

Aiden opens his arms. Hush climbs into his lap. He weighs almost nothing. Smells like cave moss. Buries his small grumpy face against Aiden's chest and shudders.

They sit. Ripple watches from the middle of the cave, quiet.

Then Pegasus says something Aiden didn't expect.

"Aiden. Listen. The Hush has been alone for five hundred years. *Five hundred.* He cannot come back with us. He is bound to this cave. The door only opens because *he* opens it. If he leaves, the door closes forever, and the cave seals up, and no other lost creature will ever be found again."

Aiden looks down at Hush. Hush is so quiet against his chest.

"But." Pegasus's voice is very gentle. "He could come if *I* stay."

Aiden looks up.

"A unicorn for a doorkeeper. Magic for magic. The cave would stay open. The Hush would get to come with you. But I would have to stay."

Aiden's eyes fill up. He doesn't want to choose. He wanted Pegasus to come home so badly. He has not let himself think about anything else for an hour.

Pegasus lowers her head. "It's your choice, friend. I will honor either one."

The cave is very quiet.

Aiden looks at Hush. He looks at Pegasus. He looks at Ripple in the middle of the room.

He thinks for a long minute. Longer than seven-year-olds usually think about anything.

Then he says, quietly: "Pegasus, you said I was exactly right. I think being exactly right means I have to not be selfish today."

Pegasus closes her eyes.

Aiden says, "But — can I think for one more second?"

Pegasus says, "Take all the seconds."

Aiden thinks. He looks at Hush. Then he says: "Hush. Could you come for one day? Just one? To help us at the dock-up-above? And then come back? Would that work?"

Hush sits up. He looks at Pegasus. Pegasus looks surprised, then *delighted.*

"You — you could leave for ONE DAY?" Pegasus asks Hush. "Not forever?"

Hush considers. "One day is forty-eight hours less than five hundred years. I think I can do one day."

Pegasus says, "Then the door stays open for that day. Magic permits a visit. It does not permit a move."

Aiden laughs. He has solved a problem that wasn't supposed to have a solution.

Hush attaches to Aiden's shoulder. He has gone from sad-grumpy to *brave* in the space of about three minutes. He sits up straighter on Aiden's shoulder than he has sat in five centuries. He even tries a small spike-stretch — for the first time in his life, he wants to be slightly more impressive.

"Look," he whispers to Aiden, awed at himself. "I am — I am DOING things. I never DO things. You made me do things."

Aiden says, matter-of-factly, "You did. You were always going to. You just needed someone to ask."

Hush thinks about that for a long moment. It is the kind of moment a small creature has when something gets rearranged inside him forever.

Pegasus bumps Aiden's forehead with her horn.

"You found the third option," she says. "Most adults can't. Most children can. You just did. And look — you made him brave just by treating him like he already was."

Aiden grins. He didn't think he had made anyone brave. But he had.

[CROSSOVER HOOK: *Above the cliff, a small flat stone skips across the sky in a perfect mirrored pattern. Hush tilts his small grumpy head. "Sometimes things fly here that shouldn't. I never ask why."*]

They surface in the meadow. Ripple's mother weeps. Hush rides Aiden's shoulder. Pegasus flies them home.

**→ Exit to P-186.**

## P-185 — PATH C: THE RIVER'S BEND (HIDDEN — A HARDER CHOICE)

*~700 words. Yields Pegasus + Canyon Singer confirmation + a real cost paid up front.*

Pegasus flies them to the River's Bend. The river is wide and warm. The otters have been waiting.

A tea party on a flat rock in the middle of the river. Tiny driftwood table. Teapot the size of a thimble. Ripple's mother is there — calm. The otters have been keeping her company.

"We knew you would come. Have tea before you rescue her."

Aiden looks at Pegasus. Pegasus shrugs. "We have a few minutes."

But the head otter — a small grey-muzzled creature wearing a kelp necklace, who introduces herself as Aunt Stream — does not look like a creature offering small talk.

"Child. Before we begin. There is something you need to know about the river. The rescue is not free."

Aiden sets his thimble of tea down.

"To open the hole, someone has to give the river something that matters. Not gold. Not magic. *Something that matters to you.* That is the river's price."

Aiden looks at Pegasus. Pegasus is suddenly very still.

"What do I have that matters?"

Aunt Stream looks at him kindly. "You have the unicorn."

Aiden's chin starts to wobble.

"No. Not her staying. Not her belonging to you. The river will not take a creature. The river takes a *promise.* Pegasus is a unicorn. Unicorns belong to the whole world, not to one boy. If you bring her home, you have to promise that she gets to come back. Not just to visit. She gets to come *back* — when the work at your lake is done. She does not stay with you forever."

Aiden looks at Pegasus. Pegasus is looking back at him.

He says, in a very small voice, "When does she come back?"

"When she's ready. Maybe a year. Maybe ten. Maybe when you are a grown-up. The river does not say. The river just asks for the promise."

Aiden thinks about this for a long time. The otters are quiet. Ripple's mother is quiet. The river is louder than it had been.

He says, "I will promise."

Pegasus says, "Aiden."

"It's right. You belong to everyone. You're a UNICORN. You waited two hundred years for me. I can wait some years for somebody else to need you next. You should get to find them."

Pegasus puts her horn against his forehead. Her eyes are wet.

Aunt Stream says, "Good. The river accepts."

The river thrums. The rescue hole opens itself underneath them.

Then Aunt Stream gets a little quieter. "Child. While we have you. Do you ever hear singing? At night? At your lake-up-above?"

Aiden thinks. "Yes. Two summers ago. A LADY was singing. We never knew who. Mom said it was nobody."

Aunt Stream nods, slowly. "It was *somebody.* Your mother is shyer than she pretends. Pass it on, but gently. The singer should know we heard her. When the time is right. Not yet."

Aiden tucks that away in the part of his brain that holds important things.

They dive. They find Ripple. They carry her up.

Pegasus and Aiden fly home.

On the way back, Pegasus says: "Aiden. You did the bravest thing of all eight of you today. You let me go before I went."

Aiden, quietly: "I didn't let you go. I let you visit other people. That's different."

Pegasus laughs the loudest unicorn laugh in two hundred years.

[CROSSOVER HOOK: *Above the river, the basalt cliffs are visibly streaked with color — blue, orange, green — that wasn't there an hour ago. "The painter girl woke them," Aunt Stream says. "Big day."*]

**→ Exit to P-186.**

## P-186 — EXIT

*~200 words. Always read.*

The sky is purple-gold. The C-130 is rumbling somewhere up ahead, calling them home.

[VARIANT: PATH A. *Pegasus flies smoothly. Aiden grips her mane. Ripple is safe with her mother. Aiden has done his part.*]

[VARIANT: PATH B. *Pegasus flies. Hush is on Aiden's shoulder, hanging on with both small paws. He whispers "LOOK at the SKY" every few minutes because the sky is the most beautiful thing he has ever seen and he wants to make sure Aiden notices.*]

[VARIANT: PATH C. *Pegasus flies. Aiden hears, faintly under the wind, the start of a song coming from below — Aunt Stream's promised song, the lake carrying it. He smiles. He won't tell anyone yet. He's been told to wait.*]

He has Pegasus. He has, maybe, a friend on his shoulder, or a song in his ears, or just a unicorn at his back.

He always has Pegasus.

Pegasus says, "LOOK AT THIS!"

She is pointing at the sun coming through the canyon.

Aiden says, "Yeah."

**→ Region complete.**

## Editor notes

**On preserving the best v2 lines:** Kept verbatim: "I picked it MYSELF," "LOOK AT THIS!", "I have been waiting for a rider for two hundred years," "We'll all be unicorns. We just won't have horns. We'll just be... unicorns INSIDE." Those lines exist nowhere else and shouldn't be touched.

**On Pegasus always crossing:** Locked. Per the crossover rule, Pegasus is the one ally who *always* comes back with the kid. Aiden's branch is the engine of the whole book. Even on Path A (the easy path) Pegasus still comes home. This is non-negotiable.

**On "the exactly-right moment" (P-181):** Aiden's cared-for beat. Pegasus tells him — directly, before the quest starts — *you are not too small. You are not too young. I picked you. The world is going to tell you you're too little for the rest of your life. The world is going to be wrong.* This is the most important thing the book says to its youngest reader. Worth being explicit.

**On Path C (Canyon Singer confirmation):** This is the deepest payoff for the whole book's easter egg. Aunt Stream confirms what Madison's Path C suggested: *the lake has been carrying that voice across the worlds. Your mother is shyer than she pretends.* This is the most explicit the book ever gets about the Canyon Singer. It STILL doesn't name names. But the kid reading it will land on the answer, and any adult reader who knows the family lore gets the full reveal here.

**On the otter family connection (Path C):** Aunt Stream is Otto's cousin. The Driftwood Players are family. The otter network across The Old Roar is real and connected, which echoes the chosen-family theme one more time before the showdown. This also sets up Aiden being proud of Chayten — a small reciprocal beat to Chayten being proud of him in her region.

**On crossovers:** Path A: Reid's tree-being walking (bells ring a deep chord). Path B: Kellen's stones skipping across the sky (a small cool image). Path C: Keira's color canyon (visible above the river). Same rotation.

**On word count:** Total ~2,600.
